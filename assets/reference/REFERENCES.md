# THE HANGAR REFERENCES, AND WHAT EACH ONE SAYS

Seven images in `misc/`. This file exists because a reference nobody opened is
not a reference — the first dressing of the flight deck was built from a survey
that said the parts existed, and the parts were a street lamp and a brick wall.
`tools/checks/refcheck.mjs` refuses to pass unless every file here is named
below.

Read the images. Do not read only this file.

---

## WHAT ALL SEVEN AGREE ON — the rules, in order of how much they matter

### 1. THE APERTURE HAS A THICK, BRILLIANT WHITE RIM, and it is the hero
Every single reference. The opening is a rounded rectangle (1, 4, 5) or a
chamfered hexagon (7), and its whole perimeter is a continuous glowing white
band — the brightest thing in frame by a wide margin, brighter than anything
lit by it. It reads as containment field emitters and it is what says *the
vacuum is right there*.

**What I built instead:** three flat translucent shield planes with no rim at
all, and an open top. The field was invisible in the first render because
nothing framed it. **The rim is not decoration; it is the single most
identifying element in the whole genre and it was missing.**

### 2. THE FLOOR IS A BLACK MIRROR
All seven. Near-black, glossy, and it reflects the light strips and the ships
as long vertical smears — in 5 the reflection is half the image. It is not a
grey deck with panel lines; it is a dark polished surface that doubles every
light in the room.

**What I built instead:** a matte blue-grey terrain heightfield with a faint
tile pattern, which is why the first render had "no detail on the ground".

### 3. THE SIDE WALLS ARE RANKS OF SHIP BAYS RECEDING TO A VANISHING POINT
This is what "rows and rows of ships" actually looks like (7, 6, 3, 4): fighters
racked in ANGLED WALL ALCOVES, dozens of them, marching back into haze — not
parked on the floor. The floor stays largely clear; the ships are stored in the
walls. 7 has two full walls of them.

### 4. LIGHT IS THIN BRIGHT STRIPS, AND THERE ARE NO LAMPS
Vertical bars set into the wall bays in ranks (1, 3, 5, 6); long horizontal runs
along the top of a wall (3, 4); the aperture rim. Not one visible point fixture,
not one pole, not one lamp head anywhere in seven images.

### 5. SCALE COMES FROM PEOPLE BEING TINY
In 5 the troopers are 15 px tall and there are a thousand of them. In 7 they are
specks at the foot of the racks. In 1 a squad beside the Falcon is a smudge. The
room is 40–60 m to the overhead. **Humans are the ruler and they must be small.**

### 6. THE PALETTE IS MONOCHROME BLUE-GREY, AND THE ACCENTS ARE RED
Desaturated steel-blue everything, white light, and small red accents — status
lamps on hanging fixtures (7), thin red painted outlines on the deck (5), red
markers (6). **There is no yellow anywhere in any of the seven.**

**What I built instead:** caution-yellow hazard chevrons on every edge, yellow
landing circles and a yellow trench border. I invented all of it. It is a
terrestrial airfield convention and it is in none of the references.

### 7. DECK MARKINGS ARE LARGE, PALE AND SPARSE
Big geometric zones and long lines (1's pale chevron shapes, 7's outlines), or
thin red rectangles (5). Never busy. No numerals anywhere in any reference.

**What I built instead:** 2.6 m stencilled bay numerals, concentric landing
circles with tick marks, dashed taxi lines. Too busy, wrong colour, and the
numerals are pure invention.

### 8. OVERHEAD: HANGING FIXTURES WITH RED STATUS LIGHTS
Massive cylinders dropping from above with red lamp clusters (7), gantry beams
and cable runs (3, 4), docking clamps (4). They come down from out of frame,
which is how the top of the room reads as enormous without ever showing a
ceiling — the trick I already had and the only one I got right.

## PER IMAGE

### hangar 1.webp — Death Star docking bay, the Falcon
The canonical wide shot. Black mirror deck with huge pale chevron/arrow
markings. Aperture: white rounded-rect rim, stars beyond. Right wall canted
outward with a rank of tall vertical white strips. A lit recessed pit in the
floor. Troopers tiny beside the ship. **Take:** the aperture rim, the deck
reflectivity, the canted wall of strips, the recessed pit.

### hangar 2.webp
See 1 — same bay, second angle. **Take:** confirms the wall rhythm and rim.

### hangar 3.jpg — TIE bay, blue-lit
The bluest reference. Racked TIEs on gantry mounts overhead and on the deck, a
rank of troopers standing in formation mid-ground, an R-unit and a crew chest.
Long horizontal light runs at the top of the wall; vertical strips below.
**Take:** the exact blue, the overhead TIE mounts, and that a formation of men
in the middle distance is the subject — this is the closest reference to what
this room is FOR.

### hangar 4.jpg — TIE bay looking out at a moon
Aperture is a wide rounded rect with a fat white rim; a planet and duelling
fighters visible through it. Deck markings are pale dashed lines in long
straight runs. Wall bays angled, each with its own strip. A servicing gantry
with an access platform beside a TIE. **Take:** the deck marking style (long
pale dashes, sparse), and that the view through the aperture carries the frame.

### hangar 5.webp — the muster
**The reference for this room's whole purpose.** A thousand troopers in ranks on
a black mirror deck, officers in front, a shuttle landing behind them, the
aperture's white rim across the back. Thin red painted rectangles on the deck.
Right wall a receding rank of angled bays with white strips. **Take:** the
formation as subject, the reflectivity, the red accents, and the ranks reading
as texture at distance.

### hangar 6.jpg — the wide grey bay
Almost desaturated. TIEs on overhead racks down both walls, a catwalk across the
back at mid-height with a single figure on it for scale, vertical strips in
ranks. Two crew in the near corner. **Take:** the catwalk-for-scale, the
symmetry, and how dark this is allowed to be.

### hangar 7.jpg — concept art, the closest to our composition
Chamfered hexagonal aperture with a thick glowing rim, stars and TIEs beyond. A
shuttle on a pad, wings folded up. TWO FULL WALLS of racked TIEs receding to a
vanishing point. A lit recessed pit. Lines of troopers as specks. Hanging
cylindrical overhead fixtures with red status lamps. Crate clusters. **Take:
this is the target composition.** Everything else is detail.

## WHERE OURS DIFFERS ON PURPOSE

The player's brief: *"ours will have space/force field on 3 sides and the
ceiling, scale must be immense, rows and rows of different ships, troopers,
droids, ships coming in and out, battle in background"*.

So the references' single opening in a solid wall becomes **field on three sides
and overhead**, with the aft bulkhead the only solid surface. That is MORE open
than any reference and it is the point — but the rim is what makes a field read
as a field, so every field edge gets one: where a reference has a rim around a
window, ours has a rim around each field plane and along the deck lip.
