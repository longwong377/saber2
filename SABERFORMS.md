# THE THREE SABER FORMS — the design, settled

Two new weapons built as extra `Saber` INSTANCES driven through the public `setHiltPose` + `update` contract that Enemy, Net, Menu and the thrown blade already drive — `Saber.js` and `Combat.js` get ZERO changed lines — where the staff buys a wider GUARD ROSE (which is where a planted, guarding player actually answers bolts) and the pair buys a second SWEPT QUAD (which is where a moving, swinging player answers them), because `Bolts.update` asks the rose per OWNER and the sweep per ENTRY, and that one asymmetry in shipped code is the whole difference between the two playstyles.

## The saberstaff

SABERSTAFF — two Saber instances sharing one hand and one hilt transform, pommel-to-pommel.

The lower Saber is built from the SAME `hiltStyle` the player picked, posed at the same hand point with a π flip about the hand's forward axis and offset down the shaft by twice that hilt's own measured `hiltFloor()`. Two Graflexes back to back is 0.490 m emitter to emitter and 2.79 m tip to tip. Every hilt in the rack becomes a staff variant for free — no new `HILT_SPECS` row, so the table's ±15 mm `emitter` bound and its ten-row silhouette check are untouched. Both hilts are DRAWN, which is what makes the silhouette read as a saberstaff rather than as one blade with a bug.

Blade length is the player's own `bladeLength`, read twice. That is the only home "more reach" gets: already per-Saber, already a Net LOOK key, already clamped against the session ceiling. `armMax`, `GRIPS.guardR` and `GUARD.radius` are BODY measurements and none of them moves.

`control.grip = 'staff'` — a new GRIPS row at inertia 1.34 with kD re-solved at the file's own ζ 0.60 — and `handsOnHilt()` still answers 2, because a saberstaff genuinely is two hands on one hilt. The hilt is just longer.

Its defensive identity is ROSE ARC. `_publishGuard`'s `g.half` gains one per-instance addend, `setHalf = asin((span/2) / GUARD.radius)` ≈ 10.1° on a Graflex, computed from the built weapon rather than typed, and clamped under the shipped `GUARD.sector + GUARD.tolerance` ceiling so no difficulty tier can be pushed past 135°. `GUARD.sector` stays 45° so the four zones still tile the rose exactly and `guardZoneOf` still classifies against four fixed bearings — `directional.mjs`'s twin check is untouched.

Its offensive identity is that the far end is rigidly opposite the near one, so a level sweep crosses the ring TWICE per pass with no second press, no second input and no new animation. That is the player's "second blade instantly ready" delivered by geometry. On top of it the light cut repeats faster (cooldown 0.22 against 0.30) over a narrower arc (0.60/0.62 against 0.80/0.82), so each contact is softer and they come more often.

And its `throw` key is the telekinetic spin.

## Dual wielding

JAR'KAI — two independent Saber instances, main in the right hand at the player's own `bladeLength`, off in the left at 0.80× of it (0.92 m on a stock blade, so it reads as a shoto and the pair's claim is BEARINGS rather than metres).

The off blade is a real second `_bladeEntries` record carrying `guard: null, screen: null`, so unlike the enemy's shipped dualist it genuinely deflects, is genuinely graded through `captureSnapshot` → `gradeCaught`, and genuinely bills stamina on the identical four rungs off the identical one bar.

`control.grip = 'pair'` — a new GRIPS row in the one-handed family (kP 132, inertia 0.80, kD 14.9 at ζ 0.58, matching `rev`) — and `handsOnHilt()` answers 1, which is what is true: one hand on the main hilt and the other on its own. "Which weapon is in which hand" gets a NEW reader, `offHandOn()`, beside it rather than a term inside it.

There is ONE SaberController. The off blade is not independently aimed: its guard is the main guard mirrored about the body's right axis and dropped, and during an attack it is driven along the main blade's own arc through `lag = clamp((u - 0.35) / 0.65, 0, 1)` — Enemy's shipped, measured rule ported verbatim, so the two blades read as two weapons arriving on two beats rather than one weapon drawn twice.

Its defensive identity is a SECOND SWEPT QUAD. That is worth almost nothing while you stand and hold the guard, because the rose has already consumed the bolt, and worth a great deal while you are moving and swinging with the guard down, because then the swept quads are the only thing answering at all. The pair is the form that covers you while you are busy.

Its offensive identity is the second body. Both blades run through ONE `BladeContactSolver` instance, so the shipped progress key `target.id + ':' + cap.name` and its 0.14 s per-capsule cooldown make the off blade worth ZERO against one man — its contact lands inside the main blade's cooldown on the same capsule — and full value against the second. Two men in the cone take both blades; one man takes one.

And it is the only form that can throw one and keep one.

## The trade, both halves

THE STAFF GIVES UP THE FORCE, THE CONTACT, AND THE FLANKS' PRICE.

It cannot go one-handed at all. `wantOne` is five clauses — a live telekinetic grip on a body or an enemy, a stasis field, a carried crate, a thrown blade, the toggle — and the moment any of them goes true a saberstaff has to retract one end, because two hands on one shaft is what it IS. The single blade pays for those with a looser grip (handExtend 0.29 → 0.36, guardR 0.60 → 0.72); the staff pays for them with half its weapon. It is the only form that forces a choice between the Force and the sword, and that choice is live in every fight where you want to hold something.

Its contacts are softer. A narrower arc is a slower tip, measured on SLASH's own width table (0.65/0.67 peaked 13.2 m/s, 0.80/0.82 peaked 16.9), so the staff lands around 12.6 where the single blade lands at 16.9. Severance is a work budget against TOUGHNESS, so the staff ties the single blade's damage rate against a soft body (0.75× per contact × 1.37× the presses) and loses outright against an armoured one, and it clears `SPEED_GRADE.perfect` (9.4 m/s) with less margin on every deflection.

And its wider rose is billed automatically at full price. `setHalf` +10.1° is +13.5% of all directions at Knight, and every one of those extra bolts costs the shipped `GUARD_COST.stamina` — so the staff answers more and runs dry sooner, which moves its suppression crossover DOWN from ~28 rifles toward ~25. The staff is the first form that can be pinned by a smaller firing line. Against a line directly in front the gain is near zero anyway (103.5° already covers the whole frontal hemisphere) and the bill is what is left, so a staff standing head-on into massed fire is strictly worse than a single blade. Its coverage is for being FLANKED, and it is priced whether or not you are.

Its guard also settles 14% slower — ω 10.79 against 12.49 — so it enters zones later and clears the 0.20 s PARRY window less often, which costs it the two free rungs more than any other form.

DUAL WIELD GIVES UP THE HEAVY, THE BIND, AND EVERY SECOND MAN IT IS NOT FIGHTING.

It has no heavy and no charge, not by a flag but by construction: those are two-handed moves with a chamber and the pair has no second hand. Its third press is two 15.6 m/s contacts instead of one 22.0 m/s chop, which is the move that parts armour. It cannot bind either — `resolveBladeClash` is still offered `p.saber` against `e.saber` once per pair, so the off blade cannot parry, chamber, win a lock or break a guard. In a duel the pair is a form carrying a second weapon that is not in the exchange, and the duel is won by the exchange: `World.GUARD_COST` 22 × `TIER.guardBreak` 0.6/1.9/3.2, not by damage rate.

Against ONE body its second blade is worth exactly zero. Both blades run through one `BladeContactSolver` instance, and the shipped progress key `target.id + ':' + cap.name` carries no blade identity with a 0.14 s per-capsule cooldown, so the off blade's contact lands inside the main blade's own cooldown on the same capsule and is refused. That is deliberate, it is the whole balance of the form, and it must not be "fixed".

Its defensive gain is narrow and situational. While the guard is HELD, the second entry adds almost nothing — the rose is owner-scoped and deterministic, so it already answered or already declined on entry one. The pair's coverage only exists while the guard is DOWN and the blades are moving, which is exactly when a dual wielder is exposed. And every extra bolt it does answer is graded and billed at full price on the same one bar, with `guard: null` and `screen: null` on that record so the off blade opens no auto-guard cone, covers no man beside you, and cannot re-arm the cone either.

Its off blade is on the wrong side of the disarm rule: an arm hit takes the shoto first, and the pair spends the rest of that fight as a worse single blade.

And throwing it converts you, for about five and a half seconds, into the shortest single blade in the game on the loosest of the three grips.

Its cut is the price of its feet. The pair walks 8% faster at every pace than either two-handed set, and the 0.02 of arc that comes off `DUAL_SLASH.rise` for it is a measured 10% off the cut work it banks against four bodies at once — faster feet, softer hands, in the currency the envelopes are written in. The bound `saberforms: each set is a trade` now holds is an exchange rate: a set may keep no more of the single blade's arc than the pace it was given leaves it. And its wider shoulder line is on the same throw-away key as everything else it owns: `setReach` is gated on `offHandOn()`, so for the five and a half seconds the shoto is in the air the pair guards inside a single blade's 100° like everybody else.

**And one number in this document turned out to be inert, which is worth more than the tuning.** `DUAL_SLASH.cooldown` 0.26 was chosen to put the pair's cadence at 3.85 presses a second between one blade's 3.33 and the staff's 4.55. It does not: `SaberController.applyInput` gates a light cut on `slashCool <= 0 && thrustCooldown <= 0`, every light press also opens the stab, and that line sets `thrustCooldown = SLASH.cooldown / attackRate` — **the SINGLE BLADE's 0.30, in every set**. So any set whose own `slash.cooldown` is at or under 0.30 repeats at 0.30. Measured on the pace bench, ten seconds of mashing: cooldown 0.26 gives 25 accepted presses, 0.30 gives 25, 0.45 gives 13. The pace was charged to the cadence first and the price measured as nothing, which is how this was found. The masking is left standing because the fix lives in a line that reads `SLASH` for all three sets and moving it moves the saberstaff's tempo, which is measured and green.

## The staff's moves

- **Level sweep (light cut)** — **input**: thrust — Mouse1 / PadY, binding unchanged · **does**: STAFF_SLASH: rise/drop 0.60/0.62, cooldown 0.22, chain 0.72. Narrower and 36% faster to repeat than SLASH, and BOTH ends cross the arc on one press because the far blade is rigidly opposite the near one. Selected by turning the existing `const T = this.isHeavy ? HEAVY : SLASH` expression at :1642 into a table lookup whose default row is that same shipped pair.
- **Whirl (third press of the chain)** — **input**: thrust held after two light cuts · **does**: STAFF_HEAVY: flatter and lower than HEAVY (lift 0.10 / fall 0.62 against 0.30/0.75), because the second end covers the high line for free. Reads HEAVY.hold / full / cutScale / rec / drain and the whole CHARGE block unchanged — only the arc differs.
- **Butt strike** — **input**: attackStab — WheelDown / PadDown · **does**: The near end drives forward while the far end stays back: the one move a single blade physically cannot make. Its own `_slX`/`_slY` offset pair, because the note at SaberController:1860 records what happened last time an offset was interpolated from a guard that still carried it (|gx| reached 1.0e137 in five seconds).
- **Full whirl** — **input**: attackSpin — Mouse4 / PadBack+PadB · **does**: SPIN's shipped envelope with both ends live: 2.6 body revolutions with 2.30 m of metal on the ring instead of 1.15. Same cooldown, same stamina, roughly twice the bodies crossed. Reuses SPIN's `yaw`/`turns` shape and therefore inherits `Cloak.carry`, which is what stops a fast body spin straightening the cape into a plank.
- **Half-stance** — **input**: grip2 — Backquote / PadBack+PadRB, the existing one-hand toggle · **does**: Retracts the lower end. The staff fights as a single blade on GRIPS.one, deliberately — a player who needs a hand free CHOOSES to give one up rather than having it taken. Same key, same sentence it already makes everywhere else in the game.
- **Spin barrier** — **input**: throw — KeyH / PadLB+PadRB · **does**: Enters a fourth `throwState`, 'orbit'. Both blades leave the hands and are driven round the chest by `setHiltPose`, exactly as the flying disc already is — about the shaft's own middle, on a disc whose plane walks round the body once per hold, and answering every bearing through the guard descriptor rather than a wedge in front of you. `grip` promotes it to the existing `piloted` state at reticle distance, so the staff keeps the whole throw suite. See spinBarrier.

## Dual wielding's moves

- **Alternating cut** — **input**: thrust — Mouse1 / PadY · **does**: DUAL_SLASH: rise/drop 0.74/0.76, cooldown 0.26, chain 0.62 — between the single blade and the staff. The off blade follows on the same arc mirrored about the body's right axis through Enemy's `lag`, so a press is one cut and one half-beat follow-up. `side` still alternates, so mashing draws a figure eight with two hands.
- **Cross (third press)** — **input**: thrust held after two light cuts · **does**: NOT a heavy. The pair holds no hilt with two hands, so it cannot chamber: `HEAVY` and `CHARGE` are unreachable in this set by construction rather than by a flag. The third press brings both blades through converging at roughly 15.6 m/s each instead of one at HEAVY's measured 22.0 m/s tapped. Severance is a work budget, so two mid contacts are worse against one armoured body and better against two soft ones.
- **Twin thrust** — **input**: attackStab — WheelDown / PadDown · **does**: Both points to the centreline on the same beat at the pair's shorter reach. The lunge envelope is unchanged; only the second blade is added.
- **Crossing spin** — **input**: attackSpin — Mouse4 / PadBack+PadB · **does**: SPIN with two blades at two radii on the ring — 2.07 m of metal across two bearings rather than 1.15 on one line. Same cooldown, same stamina.
- **Two swept quads** — **input**: passive, defensive · **does**: Not a move: the second `_bladeEntries` record. Worth almost nothing while you hold the guard, because the rose already consumed the bolt on entry one; worth a great deal while you run and swing with the guard down, because then the swept quads are all there is. The pair covers you while you are busy — which is when a dual wielder is busy.
- **Throw one, keep one** — **input**: throw — KeyH / PadLB+PadRB · **does**: Writes a NEW field, `Player.offThrow`, on the off Saber. `Player.throwState` is never touched, so all nine readers that mean 'the weapon is gone' stay literally true about the main blade. `POWER_COST.throwOff` 18 Force, 3.0 s cooldown from the catch. See throwing.
- **Sheathe the off blade** — **input**: grip2 — Backquote, the existing one-hand toggle · **does**: Retracts `offSaber` and drops to GRIPS.one. The pair's second entry leaves `_bladeEntries`, so a player who cannot afford the stamina can deliberately stop answering bolts. The pair's own answer to its own problem, on a key that already exists.

- **Shoulder line — how far round from your aim the guard answers at all** — **single**: 100° · **staff**: 100° · **dual**: 108.6° · **why**: `guardZoneAccepts` has TWO refusals and the first pass only ever moved one of them. `half` prunes bearings WITHIN the shoulder line; `reach` IS the shoulder line (`if (theta > guard.reach) return false`), and it is the one that was throwing the pair's bolts away. **Measured, sixty bolts round a full circle into a planted guard at 9 m, the world pinned identical across the three arms (`tools/_setbench.mjs`): single 20 of 60 landed, staff 18, pair 19, and all three stopped answering at 96°.** Driving the pair's `setHalf` to its 135° ceiling left it at 19 — with the four zones tiling the circle a 103.5° half already answers every bearing inside 100° that a held zone can face, so no width of rose reaches past it. `setReach` is `asin((cross/2)/GUARD.radius)` = 8.6°, the SAME measured span `setHalf` uses, on the other gate, and symmetric because `reach` is one number for both flanks. Ceiling `GUARD.reach + GUARD.centre` = 120°, so no set can grow the guard into a sphere. **After: single 20 landed, staff 18, pair 14–15 (−25 to −30%), shoulder line scanned at 2° reading 100°/100°/108°.** The SCAN is the sharp instrument and the count is the corroboration — even with every shared stream pinned the count carries about a bolt of spread, which is why the check bounds it at 85% of one blade's rather than at the 75% the measurement would support. ## Deflection

ONE RULE, THREE GEOMETRIES, AND THE SHIPPED PRICE LIST IS BYTE-IDENTICAL.

`GUARD_COST.stamina = [1.2, 0.4, 0, 0]` gains no row, no branch and no argument. `GUARD_COST.unanswered` stays 0.5. `guardCost` keeps its signature and its three precedence-ordered branches, so both call sites — at different moments in the frame — keep one rule. `_creditDeflect`'s single call site gains no per-set multiplier. `SPEED_GRADE`, `GRADE_DAMAGE`, `DIFFICULTY.assist`, `zoneTolerance`, `PARRY`, `PARRY_GRADE`, `CATCH`, `SCREEN` and `CLASH_RADIUS` are all outside the diff. Combat.js has zero changed lines.

THE FINDING THAT SETTLES THE THREE PROPOSALS. `Bolts.update` runs TWO tests per entry. The swept quad (`intersectBladeSweep`) is per-ENTRY and geometric. The guard rose is per-OWNER: line 370 reads `entry.owner.control.guard`, and `guardIntercept` is a pure function of that zone object and the bolt segment. So a second entry for the same owner re-runs an identical test on an identical input and can never answer what entry one declined. The loop `break`s on `consumed`, so the second entry can ONLY ever consume through its own swept quad. A second blade adds exactly one thing — its own geometry — and nothing else, provably.

That splits the two forms along a line the code already draws, and it is why all three source designs' "answered/s scales with total blade metres" arithmetic was wrong in the same direction:

  · While the guard is HELD, the rose dominates overwhelmingly — a 103.5° cap at 1.4 m against a ±12.5 cm quad — and `suppression.mjs` measures exactly that state. So the STAFF's `setHalf` addend is its real defensive gain and the PAIR's second entry is nearly redundant.
  · While the guard is DOWN, `g.active` is false and the rose is off entirely, so the swept quads are all there is. Then the PAIR's second quad is worth what it looks like it is worth and the STAFF's single front segment is worth nothing extra.

Staff = planted and guarding. Pair = moving and swinging. Zero new numbers produced that difference.

THREE THINGS MUST BE FIXED BEFORE ANY SECOND ENTRY EXISTS, and all three are behaviour-identical today. `World._onBoltDeflect` is handed the entry that met the bolt and then reaches for `owner.saber` — the snapshot at :5162, `bolts.hold` at :5190, the strains at :5143, :5192 and :4380, and the spark colour at :4386. Those become `entry.saber`. Today `entry.saber === owner.saber` for every record `_bladeEntries` builds, so the diff is provably inert; the moment a second entry exists it is a silent wrong-blade grading. `_updateCatch`'s `p.saber.ignition < 0.4` gate becomes "no lit blade at all". And every second record carries `guard: null, screen: null`, or `screenIntercept` runs twice per bolt against one owner-scoped screen and array order — not load-bearing today — silently decides which blade is credited with `auto`. Main blade is pushed first, always.

THE STAFF'S EXTRA ARC IS BOUGHT IN ONE PLACE. `g.half = Math.min(GUARD.sector + GUARD.tolerance, GUARD.sector + this.zoneTol + (this.setHalf ?? 0))` in `_publishGuard`. `guardZoneAccepts` reads `guard.half` per bolt while `guardZoneOf` classifies against the four fixed bearings independently, so widening `half` widens what a zone ANSWERS without changing what a zone IS. `GUARD.sector` stays 45° so the four sectors still tile the rose exactly and `directional.mjs` still holds Combat's copy of the arithmetic against the controller's.

AND THE SPIN BARRIER IS INSIDE THIS ECONOMY, NOT BESIDE IT. It is not a new absorb shape, so no bolt skips `gradeCaught`. While it orbits, `guarding` is false and the entries carry no cone and no screen, so every bolt it answers is a swept-quad contact off an undriven blade — a BLOCK at the shipped 1.2 stamina — on top of 13.6 Force/s. Two bars at once, at the shipped price, with no fourth branch in `guardCost`.

## Throwing one and keeping one

IT WORKS, AND THE WHOLE TRICK IS A NEW FIELD RATHER THAN A REWRITTEN ONE.

`Player.throwState` is read by nine places, and every one of them means "the weapon in your right hand is gone": `handsOnHilt` (:3706), the `wantOne` derivation (:4228 via `saberThrown`), `_updateBlade`'s pose and heat-haze gates (:5505, :5559), the arm block's own gate (:6207), the attack gate (:7808), `swapSaber` (:10027), `_dropSaber` (:10151), `_maybeDisarm` (:11050) and `HUD.js:2430`'s active wheel border. Making a throw not empty your hands is a one-word change and it would silently re-arm the single blade in all nine.

So the pair does not touch it. `Player.throwState` stays `'held'` and goes on being true. The off blade gets `Player.offThrow` — its own four-state machine (`held | flying | returning | piloted`) read by exactly two places: `Sidearm.update` for the pose, and `_bladeEntries` so the off record disappears while the blade is in the air.

Everything else is the shipped throw, because `_updateThrow`'s body is set-agnostic once you hand it a saber, a position and a velocity: 26 m/s along `aimDir`, steering toward the reticle at `lerp(aimDir·26, dt·1.4)`, turning home after 1.5 s, the terrain skim that stops the disc burying itself, the horizontal-disc pose through `setHiltPose` + `setVisible(true)`, and the cooldown that starts when the blade is BACK IN THE HAND rather than at release — the argument that comment makes is form-independent, so the rule carries over unchanged.

PRICE: A NEW ROW, `POWER_COST.throwOff = 18`. Never a conditional price on `throw`. Powers.js's opening thirty lines are a post-mortem of exactly that drift (the HUD's private copy carried lightning at 14 against a real 30), and `hud-events.mjs` still greps each spend out of Player.js to match it against the wheel. 18 sits in the empty slot above `throw`'s 14 and below `push`'s 20, and the reason is monotone: a throw that leaves you armed is strictly the better commitment, so it costs strictly more. It takes its own `_afford`, its own wheel slot, its own Codex chip and its own UNBOUND card rather than an `UNBOUND_OF` alias, whose note says `ward` is the only alias there is. Cooldown 3.0 s against the shipped 2.2, for the same reason.

WHAT IT COSTS, AND IT IS WHAT MAKES IT A DECISION. While the shoto is out the pair loses its second `_bladeEntries` record, so it loses its entire defensive identity for the flight. `offHandOn()` returns false, the off-hand strike is gone, and `GRIPS` drops from `pair` (kP 132, guardR 0.66) to `one` (kP 118, guardR 0.72) — so for five and a half seconds you are a LOOSER single-blade fighter than a single-blade fighter, holding the game's shortest main blade. One blade in the air at a time: `throwOrRecall` for a pair throws the shoto if it is held and otherwise recalls it, and the main blade cannot be thrown until the shoto is home. One key, one meaning at a time — the same pattern `swap`, `drive`, `hurl` and `throw` already use four times over.

THE STAFF DOES NOT GET THIS. One shaft, two blades: there is nothing to keep. Its `throw` key is the orbit instead, and the ordinary throw is unreachable in that set — which is a real cost, because the single blade's 26 m/s disc is a genuine ranged answer the staff simply does not have.

## The spinning barrier

IT WORKS, AND IT IS NEARLY FREE, BECAUSE THE THROW MACHINE ALREADY IS IT.

A fourth `throwState`, `'orbit'`, reachable only by a staff, entered on the existing `throw` binding.

HANDS FREE COSTS NOTHING TO BUILD, and this is the part of the player's brief that the shipped code already answers. `handsOnHilt()` returns 0 for `throwState !== 'held'`, so the moment the orbit starts both fists come off the hilt through a reader that shipped years ago; `_openPalm` opens the left palm through the path that already exists; and all thirteen left-handed GESTURES become available with the whole arm. "Keeping your hands free to cast whatever" is delivered by a clause nobody has to write.

Per frame the orbit drives `throwPos` around the chest on a 1.7 m circle and poses BOTH Sabers with `setHiltPose`, exactly as the flying disc already poses one at Player.js:8936. Pressing `grip` promotes it to the existing `piloted` state at reticle distance, so the staff keeps the whole throw suite and the orbit is its entrance rather than a replacement.

THE CRITICAL DECISION: IT IS INSIDE THE ECONOMY, NOT BESIDE IT. The orbiting blades stay in `_bladeEntries` and in the `_resolveBlades` target sweep, so they genuinely deflect and genuinely cut through the ORDINARY paths at the ORDINARY prices. Not a new absorb shape queried before the body, not immunity, and no bolt skips `gradeCaught`. That is what keeps SCOPE §3 satisfied without a single new combat rule — and it needs no `GUARD_COST` row, because while it orbits `guarding` is false, the entries carry `guard: null, screen: null`, and so every bolt it answers is a swept-quad contact off a blade nobody drove: a BLOCK at the shipped 1.2 stamina, the most expensive rung there is.

WHAT IT COSTS, and it has to be the most expensive thing in the game:
  · 24 Force to raise — a new `POWER_COST.orbit` row, between `push` 20 and `sense` 25.
  · 13.6 Force/s to hold: `(TK.lit 9 + HOLD_COST.prop.base 7) × effort 0.85`, the exact expression `pilotThrown` bills at :8845, with the same graceful failure — run out and the staff comes home, it is not dropped. `forceChannelling` gains one OR term so the 7.5/s regen is paused.
  · 1.2 STAMINA per bolt, on the ordinary ladder. At suppression.mjs's measured answering rate that is roughly 11 stamina/s on top of 13.6 Force/s against zero Force regen: under five seconds from a full bar under fire, on two bars at once. A panic button, not a stance.
  · A HARD 2.4 s CAP on top, and it is the price the coverage below was bought with. `ORBIT.cap` shipped at 4.0 while the barrier was a 100° wedge of the sightline; it answers every bearing now, so the longest hold in the game is cut by 40%. It is also the ONLY knob that costs the player anything: at 13.6 Force/s a full bar minus the 24 to raise buys 5.6 seconds of spin, so the cap has always been what ends a hold and the Force price has never once been reached. Raising the entry price is not available — `POWER_COST` is one table and a conditional price on a key is the drift Powers.js's opening thirty lines are a post-mortem of.

IT IS A BARRIER ROUND THE BODY AND IT SHIPPED AS A WEDGE IN FRONT OF IT. `Player.bladeGuard` published `cone: GUARD.reach / 2` — a 50° half-angle off `aimDir`, the rose a man holding a hilt cannot steer past — on a weapon nobody is holding. Driven, twelve bolts a bearing at 9 m, bar refilled and ring re-raised before every shot, against the same player with the spin down:

```
bearing    0°     45°    90°   135°   180°
spin      0/12   2/12   4/12  6/12   6/12   landed
control   6/12   6/12   6/12  6/12   6/12
```

Behind you it stopped nothing at all, and `tools/_spinprobe.mjs` fired every one of its shipped shots inside ±20° of the sightline — the one bearing the ordinary held guard already covers in every set — so the check that called it a barrier was measuring the arc the feature did not need to exist for. None of `GUARD.reach`'s three reasons (shoulders, elbows, eyes) survives a staff the Force is turning while both your palms are open, so `ORBIT.cone` is π: the full sphere, with `radius` — the weapon's own `half` — left as the only thing deciding what it reaches. Measured after: 0 of 12 at all five bearings against the same 6 of 12 control. And the PLANE goes round with it, one full turn per `ORBIT.cap`, because a shell answered by metal lying in one plane for the whole hold would be a rule with no picture.

WHAT IT GIVES UP, and this is what stops it being a strictly-better button. While it orbits you have NO GUARD ROSE AT ALL — `bladeHeld` is false, `guarding` is false, `_publishGuard` returns early with `zone = NONE` and `parry = false`. No directional zone, no PARRY window, no RETURN-by-timing, no PERFECT-by-timing. The orbit answers at the worst rung there is, with no timing in it at all. The screen goes too (`_screenFor` is gated on `control.bladeHeld`), so a staff user spinning is covering nobody but himself. And with 100 Force and the stamina bar both draining, a duellist walks straight through it — the blades cannot bind, cannot chamber and cannot parry.

Bodies that walk into the circle are cut, because the segments are still real Sabers in the target sweep. That is free and it is the "keeps a crowd off you" half.

NO NEW BINDING. It rides the `throw` key conditionally: staff lit in hand → the orbit; staff retracted or already orbiting → recall. Same swap/drive/hurl/throw pattern. If it proves too strong the knob is the orbit's rate, which lowers `speedAt` for every consumer at once and therefore lowers the grade of every contact — never a damage multiplier and never a price.

## Geometry

A blade in this game is a rigid segment from `base` to `tip`, recomputed once a frame by two lines in `Saber.update` (`base.set(0, this.emitterY, 0)`, `tip.set(0, this.emitterY + len, 0)`, both through `root.matrixWorld` — verified at Saber.js:1949-1950), and every combat system reads only that segment plus its two endpoint velocities. Those two lines are shared by every blade in the build including ~30 enemy sabers, the menu preview, `toon/scene.js` and every headless check that news up a bare Saber. NEITHER NEW WEAPON TOUCHES THEM. There is no back-extent opt, no `emitterY` offset, no second quad in the shader.

THE STAFF IS TWO SEGMENTS, NEVER ONE LONG ONE, and that is the single most important structural decision here. `Saber.speedAt(t) = lerp(|baseVelocity|, |tipVelocity|, t)` (:1922) and `captureSnapshot`'s identical `bladeSpeed` (Combat.js:887) assume speed rises monotonically from a slow base to a fast tip. True of a weapon pivoting near or below its grip; FALSE of a staff spun about its middle, where both ends move at V and the centre at ~0 — the lerp would report V along the entire shaft, the grip would sever flesh as hard as the tip, and every contact would clear `SPEED_GRADE.perfect` at 9.4 m/s. Built as two segments each pivoting about its own base at the hands, the lerp is CORRECT on both halves with no change to `speedAt` for anybody, and the `hit.bladeSpeed ??` escape hatch already sitting in `captureSnapshot` is never needed. Everything bladeT-indexed then means the same thing on all four segments in the game, measured from the hand outward: `returnBladeT` 0.42, `perfectBladeT` 0.55, `bolts.hold`'s pin fraction, `intersectBladeSweep`'s return value, and `captureSnapshot`'s radial normal.

THE POSE. The staff's lower Saber is posed at the SAME hand point with `q · flip`, where `flip` is a π rotation about the hand's forward axis, then offset down the shaft by twice that hilt's own `hiltFloor()` — one `setFromAxisAngle` and one `addScaledVector` in the adapter, so the two hilts meet exactly at their pommels and the emitters come out 0.490 m apart on a Graflex. `Saber` computes both halves' endpoints from its own root exactly as it always has. Both hilts are drawn, which is what makes the silhouette a saberstaff and what makes every hilt in the rack a staff variant.

The pair's off Saber is posed from a genuine second hand target through the full arm pipeline — not `Enemy._poseOffhand`'s low-fidelity solve, which puts the WRIST on the hilt axis and never calls `handPoseOnHilt` at all. Reuse its structure; take the pose from the player's own pipeline.

WHAT GOES IN THE BLADE LIST. `_bladeEntries`'s existing player push is untouched, byte for byte, and the set branches beside it: `single` pushes nothing extra; `staff` pushes ONE extra record for whichever segment has the greater dot with `aimDir` — because `GUARD.reach` 100° already says you cannot guard behind you, and a blade eating bolts behind your own back is not a feature; `pair` pushes one for `offSaber`. Every extra record carries `guard: null, screen: null` and comes after the main one.

CUTTING: ONE `BladeContactSolver` INSTANCE PER WIELDER, and both blades solved through it. The class is already newed per caller in nineteen places across World and the checks, so this is the shipped pattern rather than a new one — and it makes the shipped progress key `target.id + ':' + cap.name`, which carries no blade identity and holds a 0.14 s per-capsule cooldown, into the mechanic: a second blade is worth zero against one body and full value against the second. Combat.js needs no key change and no edit at all.

CLASH: `resolveBladeClash` is still called once per (player, enemy) pair on `p.saber` vs `e.saber`. Neither the staff's far segment nor the pair's off blade is ever passed to it — an absence, deliberately, because the function returns ONE winner per call decided by raw speed and there is no rule anywhere in this codebase for "my off blade binds while my main blade is free". Inventing one is how a blade ends up cutting through a block.

COST PER EXTRA BLADE PER FRAME: one `ground.scar(prevTip, tip)`, one 30×3-quad trail rebuild, one tip/base velocity pair, and two `Engine.lightUp` requests at `lightPriority: 0` into a pool that is FIXED at eight precisely so `NUM_POINT_LIGHTS` cannot move and no lit material recompiles.

## The grip

FOUR SURGICAL TOUCHES IN Player.js, EACH PROVABLY THE IDENTITY AT THE DEFAULT, AND NOTHING IN Rig.js OR Bodies.js.

That last is the tripwire: if a diff for a saberstaff touches Rig.js, the problem has been solved in the wrong file. Both hands are already first-class mirrored bones with `parts`/`primary`/`radius`, `solveIK` is callable per arm per frame, `orientBoneWorld` is the public hand stamp, both hands already carry the open/closed morph, and `severanceOf` prices a lost arm off the rig rather than off a table. `Saber.setGripScale` is the lever that fits the WEAPON to the hand, and it lives in the weapon's own file.

(1) `handsOnHilt()` BECOMES A TABLE LOOKUP. `HANDS_ON = { two: 2, one: 1, rev: 1, staff: 2, pair: 1 }` with `?? 1`. For the only three grip values that exist today it reproduces `this.control.grip !== 'two' ? 1 : 2` for every reachable combination of `saberDown`, `driving`, `throwState` and `gesture.kind`. It is not a set term inside the reader — the note over it explicitly refuses that, having been burned once by an `fpHands` option that put a fist back on a hilt the game had taken a hand off — it is the same derivation from the grip model, spelled as data. A second reader, `offHandOn()`, sits beside it and answers "which weapon is in which hand", which is the question `handsOnHilt` cannot express and must not learn.

(2) `handPoseOnHilt` GAINS ONE OPTIONAL ARGUMENT, `end`, DEFAULT +1, at line 1237: `multiplyScalar((L ? 1 : -1) * end)`. `1 · x` is exact, and the sign then flows THROUGH `x` into the `applyAxisAngle(x, -BORE_LEAD)` composition at :1291 — which is the invariant the 119.6° palm repair established. GRIP_TWIST is applied about the thumb axis, which mirrors between hands; applying the flip anywhere outside that composition re-creates the exact defect that note records, and `FP_TUNE.roll` was swept AFTER the repair, so changing the composition order silently invalidates a swept constant. The staff's lower hand passes −1 because its thumb points at the lower emitter. All five external callers (Menu.js:3051 and four benches) and both in-game call sites omit it.

(3) `GRIP_AT` GAINS KEYS AND LOSES NONE. `GRIP_PAIR` R +0.050 / L −0.015 and the derived `ONE` and `FP` are byte-identical, so `hilts.mjs:126` — which asserts all three lie inside every HILT_SPECS row's measured extent with 12 mm of margin — is unchanged, and `FP_HAND_GAP`, which is measured from `GRIP_AT.R − GRIP_AT.L`, does not move. The staff adds `STAFF_R` / `STAFF_L` at roughly ±0.10, measured against the DOUBLED hilt's extent, and gets its own clause in that check. The pair's off hilt reuses `ONE`, because a shoto in one hand IS a one-handed hold. The lookup is `const G = GRIP_FOR[set] ?? GRIP_AT` — identity default, one line. `fpGripOn(saber)` already proves a grip point may be a function of the weapon and gains a set-aware return, because `hiltFloor + FIST_CLEAR` on a 0.49 m shaft puts the viewmodel fist below the lens.

(4) THE 340-LINE ARM BLOCK GETS ONE EXTRACTION AND NO NEW BRANCH. `_poseHandOnHilt(side, saber, gripY, toward, pole, dt)` doing exactly: `saber.root.localToWorld` the grip point scaled by `gripScale`; `handPoseOnHilt`; wrist = grip + boreBack; `_wristPole`; `solveIK`; `_rollForearm`; hand stamp — in that order, because the stamp must follow the IK (the note at :6450 says so, and the whole block is downstream of spine → clavicles → `rig.updateMatrices()`). The existing block calls it twice with the values it already computes. The pair calls it a third time. Its own scratch pool, never `_v9`/`_v10`, because `Enemy._poseOffhand` re-reads chest and right into `_o1.._o7` for exactly this reason. It ships as its own commit, asserted bit-identical on `viewmodel.mjs` and `first-person.mjs`, BEFORE any set exists.

THE STAFF NEEDS NOTHING BEYOND (1)–(3). `twoHanded` is already true, so the shipped block runs exactly as written with two different grip offsets and one flipped thumb. That is why the staff ships first and the pair ships second.

TWO THINGS A SET CHANGE MUST DO THAT NOTHING ELSE IN THE FILE DOES. `this._foreRoll` and `this._elbow` are per-bone signed-angle ratchets with rate limits (FOREARM.rate 40, ELBOW.rateThird 18) whose `.have` is cleared in exactly ONE place — `respawn`, :11215-11216. Moving handL between "on the shared shaft", "on its own hilt" and "at rest" is the same discontinuity a respawn is, so the set change clears them too. Do NOT raise the rates instead: both were re-swept against the third-person carry and read 1370°/s against the viewmodel bench's 1250 ratchet, and raising either re-opens the 10723°/s forearm spin that `_rollForearm`'s whole note is about. And the hand resets at :6405-6406 and :6538-6539 are the only lines in the file that put a wrist back to `restQuat`, and they are asymmetric — handL when the grip goes one-handed, handR only when the saber is on the ground — so the pair adds its own reset for handL when the shoto is thrown or sheathed, or the wrist freezes 167° off rest, which is the defect the comment at :6403 records.

CLOTH REACTS BY IMPULSE, NOT BY COLLIDERS. `this.cloak?.impulse(dir, strength)` from the whirl and the orbit, the way the jump and the landing already do. Leave `refreshColliders` alone — adding arm or weapon spheres moves the cost of every garment on the roster, and `cloth-cost.mjs` prices the whole column as particles × colliders.

## Input

ZERO NEW BINDINGS, BECAUSE THERE ARE ZERO AVAILABLE.

Measured against the shipped table with FORMATIONS registered — 62 actions, 114 codes, 0 conflicts — there are no free keyboard letters of 26, no free digits of 10, no free punctuation of 11, no free mouse controls of 7 including both wheel notches, and no usable bare pad buttons (PadLB and PadBack are deliberately unbound modifiers that `controls.mjs` asserts stay unbound). So every set move rides an existing binding whose meaning is already conditional on state — the pattern this codebase has already paid for four times over (`swap` = take-or-drop, `drive` = in-or-out, `hurl` = gripped-body-or-stasis, `throw` = throw-or-recall):

The Codex prints one line per binding row, so three rows now describe three games each — the row's `text: k => …` closure can print the live set's meaning off `this.s.saberSet`, which is what it is for. The two new `POWER_COST` rows (`orbit`, `throwOff`) each need a wheel slot and a Codex chip, or `hud-events.mjs` finds a spend with no readout.

## Choosing a form

IT IS CALLED `saberSet`, NOT `saberForm`, AND THAT IS A LOAD-BEARING DECISION RATHER THAN A PREFERENCE. `FORMS` and `FORM_KEYS` are exported from Duel.js for the five duelling styles, `Duel` picks one per duellist as `this.formKey`, and `tools/checks/forms.mjs` already exists and is entirely about them. `stance` is taken by the controller's lateral guard binding; `grip`, `style` and `weaponStyle` are all taken in Saber.js and SaberController.js. If the sweep contributes more than I think, the pair's crossover falls further than [20, 32] allows and the shoto has to shrink. That is exactly why the budget is written as a HARNESS BOUND rather than a constant, and why `offScale` is the tuning surface: it is already per-Saber, already linear in coverage, and changing it costs nothing anywhere else.

THE STAFF'S ENVELOPE NUMBERS ARE FIRST GUESSES WITH THE RIGHT SHAPE. `STAFF_SLASH` 0.60/0.62 at cooldown 0.22 is read off SLASH's own measured width table, and the shipped arcs were found by measuring eighteen bodies rather than by reasoning, so mine will move. What must not move while they do is SLASH, HEAVY, OVERHEAD and SPIN.

AND ONE NARROW HOLE I AM CHOOSING TO LIVE WITH. Both of a set's blades run through one `BladeContactSolver`, which shares the progress key and the 0.14 s per-capsule cooldown — that is the mechanic. But `taken`, the weak-point claim list, is per-solve-CALL, so two calls in one frame cannot see each other's claims and a single weak point could be billed twice on one frame. The per-capsule cooldown closes it on the next frame. It is worth knowing about; it is not worth an edit to Combat.js's hot loop, and taking that edit would cost the zero-lines claim the whole design rests on.

Finally, the thing I deliberately cut and want on the record: the staff's half-turn follow-through, where the shaft rolls through π so the far blade retraces the arc from the other side. It is the most exciting move any of the three source designs proposed and it is the one I do not believe on day one — π over a 0.115 s beat is 1565°/s against `FOREARM.rate`'s 1370, so the envelope has to bend around a rate limit that must not be raised, and the deeper version (the hands SWAPPING along the shaft, which is what a real quarterstaff does) reaches straight into `aimY`'s degeneracy where it is worst. The staff gets its two-tempo character for free instead, from the far blade being rigidly opposite the near one on every arc. The roll is a second pass, and it should be authored against `viewmodel.mjs` rather than argued for in a design.
