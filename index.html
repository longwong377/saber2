Rules of the session

Off, this is co-op: your blade, your bolts and your Force pass through the people you came with, and the only thing you can hurt is the horde. On, it is a free-for-all — friendly fire is not a separate switch, it is what this one means, so there are no allies and no accidents. Everybody drops to the duel's health. Takes effect on the next Ignite, and it is read on each machine, so agree on it before you deploy.

Control

Held, the blade stays where your last flick left it instead of easing back to the ready guard. The controller has always been able to do this and nothing could ask it to.

The Force

Drain at 0 is unlimited Force. Power scales reach, lift and impulse together.

The Holocron

Earned is the game: Insight is a run currency, you kneel to spend it, and nothing carries over. The other two are for looking at what is in here — a whole run can end without ever meeting Force lightning or Compel, and a power nobody can reach is a power nobody can tell you is broken.

Your army

How much of your own damage reaches your own troops. The physics is never scaled — a push throws your sergeant exactly as far as it throws a B1, a grip lifts him, and the blade goes through him whatever this says. What this scales is the damage that lands. At 0 you can ragdoll the whole line into the air and lose nobody; at 100% a careless sweep costs you a name off the roster.

"I should be able to choose to spawn in allied troops on any map in any mode if I so wish." Allies used to be a property of the mode — the roster, the ranks, the permadeath, the morale and the seven formations were reachable from Command, Skirmish and Campaign and from nowhere else, on one planet. This brings a contingent into any other mode, on any ground: the same named troopers, the same orders on the same keys, the same gone-for-good when they fall. It is not a campaign — there are no areas, no muster screen and no victory card, and the mode you picked still ends the way it always did. The wave is composed against the army standing in it, so ten men buy you a bigger fight rather than an easier one, and losses are made good slowly: one replacement every two waves you clear, never past the number you set here. 0 is off, and Command, Skirmish and Campaign ignore this entirely — their army is the mode.

Command only — a meeting needs a second commander and no other mode has one, so this is ignored in Skirmish and Campaign rather than leaving them with nobody to fight. Needs a session, and it changes what Command is. Instead of your army against a composed horde across five areas, it is one battle against another player's army — two rosters, two armies, deployed a hundred and twenty metres apart on the plain and walking toward each other. Two Jedi do not both get the Republic; whoever joined first keeps the army their order names and the other takes the opposing one. Permadeath is the same, which is the point: the field belongs to whichever side still has somebody standing, and every name you lose taking it is gone.

The battle

Skirmish is one battle with an ending, fought over changing ground — as many engagements as you say, on a different theatre each time, drawn from the run's seed so the same number walks the same grounds. Pressure is which rung of the Geonosis advance the fight is held at: it moves the enemy's budget, how heavy they come and what your muster can buy. Your line starts at the ten the campaign opens with, because that is the smallest army the muster knows how to raise — the slider cannot offer a number the battle would refuse. An engagement is a battle for one piece of ground and takes as many cleared waves as you say, so the two top sliders together are how long the whole thing is: three and three is the shipped nine-wave battle, nine and eight is a seventy-two-wave grind on nine grounds.

Standing order

The formation your squads form up in at the start of every area. You can change it mid-fight with the order keys — they are ordinary rebindable controls, listed under Key bindings and printed on the controls page.

Key bindings

Click a key to rebind. Mouse buttons work too.

Fidelity

Both multiply the tier above rather than replacing it, so Performance at 150% particles is still thinner than Cinematic at 100%. Particles take effect on the next impact; grass on the next deploy, because a field is planted when the level loads.

Severed limbs, cut props and shattered walls all share this budget; past it the oldest piece is culled. It is the number that decides whether a long fight leaves a battlefield behind it or a clean floor.

Off, reinforcements are flown, walked or dropped in and you can see them coming. On, they simply appear — cheaper, and the honest choice on a machine that cannot afford the craft.

Frame time, 1% low and draw calls in the corner of the HUD — and the graphics adapter and build id in the footer of this screen. Off by default because they are instruments: a title screen that opens with a driver string is a bug report.

Both are separate from Camera shake on purpose: with motion feedback off they are the only thing on screen that tells you a boss has arrived, or that you died.

A kill, a blow taken and a deflection each drive the pad. A slider rather than a box because rumble is the one piece of feedback here with a physical strength — 0 is silent and 1 is what the game was tuned to give. Camera shake off silences the pad as well; this scales what survives it.

Audio

Voice lines

Nothing here is a recording either way: Spoken is your browser's own speech synthesiser saying the same lines the larynx says wordlessly, which is real words at no download on a game that ships no audio but the score.

Every Force power has three or four lines of its own and never says the same one twice running — a shove falls, a pull rises, a rend is wrenched down almost an octave. Nothing here is a recording. Every voice is built from an oscillator, two formant filters and a breath of noise at the moment it is needed — which is why they can be told apart by measuring them, and why turning the last box off is worth a few frames on a slow machine.

Your voice

Your Jedi grunts with the effort of a real swing, takes a hard landing badly, and has something to say about a kill, a run of deflections, a boss walking in and being nearly dead. Five larynxes, five throats, five tempos.

Reticle

It still turns red when something is close enough to kill you — a colour that could hide that would be a customisation which removes information.

Interface

Killstreaks, deflection runs and a boss arriving, in the score column of the HUD.

Contacts around you, heading-up, out to 42 m — bosses warm, allies green, and anything further away pinned to the rim so you still know it is there. Off, it costs nothing at all rather than being drawn invisible.

Names over your troops

Wherever you have troops — the crossings, and any mode at all once Allied troops above is off zero. Rank, name, health and nerve, over the head of the man carrying them.

On, the map is what Force sense shows you: hold the power open and it stays lit while the pool drains, or tap it for a reading that fades over a few seconds. It costs what the power costs and not a Force more. Off, it is simply always there — which is what it was, and is the answer if managing a power mid-fight is not something you want to do.
