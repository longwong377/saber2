# COMPANIONS — the design, settled

One body that is yours in every mode without ever being on the roll — it owns its own targeting, it accepts the orders its bond with you has licensed and refuses the rest out loud, it is always slower than you are, and it is gone if you leave it behind.

## The relationship

Four beats, and every one of them is a cost rather than a gift.

**1. IT IS THE ONLY THING THAT IS YOURS IN ALL ELEVEN MODES.** Nine of them give you no army: the order wheel is dark, `#roster` is hidden by `HUD.setRoster` on a null summary, and you fight alone. The companion is the one body that is on your team in the duel, the dojo, the sandbox, a bare roguelite and the campaign alike. That is what the Levy targeting seam buys and it is the whole reason it is worth building — it converts nine solo modes into a two-body tactical problem without touching the army brain.

**2. IT IS ALWAYS SLOWER THAN YOU AND NOTHING EVER CHANGES THAT.** Every kind declares `pace` as a fraction of your walk, capped at 0.85 of your sprint. No rung raises it, no temper raises it, no kind exceeds the cap. You dash at 15.5 m/s for 0.24 s; it does not. Every good decision you make with your own body — break left, cross the open, take the high line — leaves it behind in the fire. This one line is the entire "less mobile than you, so protecting them is another thing to worry about" mechanism, and the check pins the cap so it cannot be tuned away by somebody trying to make the companion feel better.

**3. YOUR ARMY ALREADY LOVES IT AND IT COSTS NOTHING.** Every candidate selector in Reactions.js tests `o.team !== body.team` and none tests `o.trooper` — `findCasualty` (:802), `findPatient` (:1579), the smother branch of `chooseReaction` (:568), `startRally` (:1708). `braveryOf` has a no-record branch and `scaleOf(T,…)` is guarded `T ? … : 1`, so a recordless body answers 1.0 everywhere. A companion on your team is ALREADY a legal thing for your clones to drag out of a fire lane, patch up, cover with their own body and rally to. That is the single best gift in the whole survey and it arrives the day the first companion is fielded.

**4. AND YOU WILL KILL IT YOURSELF, AND THE GAME MUST SAY SO.** `installTeamDamage` (Command.js:4875, verified) scales the hit at the one door every injury comes through — blade, bolt, blast, fall, lightning — but it only shouts through `this.commandOf`, which the companion deliberately does not have. So the pack emits its own line, and it is a DIFFERENT line from CHECK YOUR FIRE, because cutting your own dog in half is not the same event as clipping a trooper. It names the animal by the name you gave it.

What makes it read as a mini-player rather than a turret: it has exactly one thing you have — a target you chose — and none of the things that keep you alive. It cannot dash, cannot block, cannot push, cannot heal itself. It is you with the survival mechanics removed, which is precisely why you have to be its survival mechanic.

And it remembers. Runs, kills, grounds walked, the men it stood over, the day it first went up a ramp, and the epitaphs of the ones you already lost — so the next one you take out is standing in a slot with a name already in it. Nothing that fights reads a word of it. COMPANY.md:62: "Nothing anywhere prices knowing more."

## What grows, and why it is not a stat

**THE RANK ROWS CARRY NO NUMERIC MULTIPLIER FIELD AT ALL.** Not a compressed one, not a 1.00 one — the field does not exist, and the check asserts its ABSENCE rather than its value, because a field sitting at 1.00 is an invitation with a comment on it.

That is stricter than the law troopers live under, deliberately, and here is why the compression argument saves RANKS and does not save this. COMPANY.md defends the 1.00→1.20 ladder on two grounds: it is averaged across twenty-four bodies, and a fresh muster re-earns the whole thing inside one campaign. Neither half holds here. There is exactly ONE companion — nothing averages — and it is with you in modes that have no muster, no roster and no wave budget to tune against. `tools/checks/command.mjs:833` pins monotonicity for RANKS; the companion check pins non-existence. COMPANY.md already calls the residual trooper spread "the one live contradiction in the repository"; this feature does not add a second.

**WHAT GROWS IS THE LEASH, THE ORDER SET, AND HOW LONG AN ORDER SURVIVES YOU.**

    rung 0  STRANGE   xp 0    leash  8 m   HEEL, AWAY. Fights what reaches you or it.
                                           Drops any order the moment you leave the leash.
    rung 1  KNOWN     xp 6    leash 14 m   + WARD. A standing radius round you.
    rung 2  TRUSTED   xp 16   leash 22 m   + SEEK and the kind's own verb.
                                           Keeps what you gave it after you walk away.
    rung 3  SWORN     xp 30   leash 34 m   + HOLD. And it comes to you unbidden
                                           when YOU go down, with nothing said.

`holdsCompanion(rec, duty)` is the single reader, on the exact shape of `holds()` (Command.js:731) — one table, one reader, and nothing anywhere compares a rung index to a magic number.

**WHY THE LEASH IS NOT POWER IN A COAT.** A rung-0 companion at 8 m and a rung-3 companion at 34 m have identical health, identical damage, identical pace and identical verbs. The sworn one is not better at fighting. It is FURTHER FROM YOU, which is where companions die. The ladder increases the animal's exposure and increases how much of the map you are responsible for. It is measured in metres, so it is legible at a glance with a ring on the ground and no number on any screen, and it is the literal reading of SCOPE.md warning 2 — "rank should change what a trooper can be ORDERED to do".

**XP IS EARNED IN-RUN FROM DEEDS**, on `Trooper.award()`'s shape (Command.js:3282): +1 an area crossed with it alive and inside the leash at the transition, +1 the first time per area an order you gave actually lands, +2 it reaches you while you are downed, +2 it survives an area in which it went down and you picked it up. Gates 0/6/16/30 put rung 3 at roughly 60-70% of one long campaign. Two clauses are copied verbatim from `tools/checks/command.mjs:845-848` and DRIVEN rather than transcribed: the top rung is reachable inside one run, and not before 40% of it. That is Company.js:28-32's own amendment satisfied exactly — a thing may cross runs if a single run could have produced it unaided; persistence is a shortcut to a ceiling and never a new ceiling.

**SECOND LAYER — FOUR EARNED, TWO-SIDED, SHEDDING TEMPERS**, on the `bonded` precedent (Attributes.js:325-328, `earned: true` plus a real `sheds`). They live in the companion's own table and NOT in Attributes.js, for two verified reasons: `attributes.mjs` requires every attribute id to have a real sim consumer inside `enlistBody`, and `kindOfArmy` derives kind from an ARMY id a rancor pup does not have, so `traitsFor` would deal a hawk the clone table. Each swings only the companion's own two behaviour numbers — hold seconds and break metres — and is priced net ≤ 0 by its own check on the same `priceSwing` formula:

    HEELED   (3 runs)          returns to heel faster and from further
                               — and will no longer range to a target past that tighter leash
    SCARRED  (downed twice, lived)  slower to go down a second time in the same run
                               — and breaks off an attack when hit
    KEEN     (12 landed orders)     acts 0.4 s sooner, takes a target 25% further out
                               — which is exactly how it gets killed
    RANGING  (5 runs mostly beyond 12 m)  ward radius +50%
                               — and heel tolerance loosens, so it drifts

**THIRD LAYER, FREE AND UNPRICED:** `since`, `runs`, `areas`, `kills`, `saves`, `story` (8 lines, STORY_KEEP's cap) and the epitaphs. Nothing that fights reads a word of it. Most of the felt growth should live here, because it is the only place that costs nothing.

**REFUSED IN WRITING, in the file's own header, so the next person argues with a position instead of rediscovering the question:** no hp/damage/armour/pace transfer of any size; no second companion slot, ever earned or bought (a companion that adds a body to the line is `company.mjs`'s "rank, not headcount" defect with fur on it); no companion at the muster (REVIEW-V12 puts the muster out of bounds); no cosmetic gated behind survival — every colour and every mark is available on the first animal you ever take out, because a look you have to survive for is an unlock; and no stored number the player can spend between runs.

## The orders

- **HEEL** — **does**: Come to me and stay inside the leash. Drops every standing order, drops the focus, cancels a seek mid-swing. Unrefusable at every rung — the shipped `FORMATIONS.circle.always` precedent (Command.js:1913) is the proof an order that cannot be refused is legal. It is also what the game issues implicitly at every lifecycle boundary (deploy, area change, boarding, mounting, dismount), so a companion can never be orphaned by a transition. This is the protection verb, it is available in the first minute of the first run, and it costs a tap with no aim. · **key**: Tap `companion` (ShiftRight / PadRB+PadX) with no reticle target — or open the wheel and release inside EMOTE_DEADZONE (HUD.js:587, already returns -1 there).
- **SEEK** — **does**: Kill the body under my reticle, and keep it until it dies, leaves the leash, or I say otherwise. The argument is read from the RETICLE at wheel-close, never from the wheel — which is the answer to `RadialWheel` having no argument channel and is strictly better than a target-cycler, because the thing you are looking at is already the thing you mean. Bounded the way `cmdFocus` is bounded (Command.js:9981): inside the leash, inside this body's own reach band, and no more than a slack factor further than what it would have picked anyway. Licensed at rung 2; below that the slot is cold and its caption says why. · **key**: Tap `companion` with a hostile under the reticle, or wheel up-right.
- **WARD** — **does**: Meet anything hostile that comes within N metres OF ME — not of itself. That distinction is the whole order: it makes the companion a tripwire around the player rather than a second wanderer, and it is the brief's 'attack anything that gets within a certain range of you' said precisely. N is the kind's own number (massiff 9 m, cat 16 m, astromech 0). It is a standing order and persists with no further input. Licensed at rung 1 — the rung where it stops being an animal that follows and becomes something you have deployed. · **key**: Wheel, up-left.
- **HOLD** — **does**: Stand on this ground and meet what comes to it. Takes the reticle's GROUND point, not a body, and it does NOT follow you afterwards. This is the order that makes protecting it a genuine problem, because you gave it a place and then walked away from it — which is exactly why it is the LAST rung. A green companion physically cannot be abandoned by an order you gave it. · **key**: Wheel, down-right.
- **AWAY** — **does**: Break off, do not engage anything, come to me and get behind me. Distinct from HEEL: HEEL is a position, AWAY is a refusal to fight held until cancelled. Unrefusable at every rung, for the same reason HEEL is — the two orders that make a companion SAFER must never be ones a fresh companion cannot take. · **key**: Wheel, down-left.
- **THE KIND'S OWN VERB** — **does**: One slot, twelve meanings, resolved off `KINDS[id].verb` and never off a switch on a kind's name. SLICE (astromech turns a designated door, terminal or dead turret), SPOT (hawk climbs and paints every hostile within 60 m onto your HUD for 8 s), FLUSH (cat pounces to knock flat rather than kill, setting up your blade), BLOCK (massiff interposes and eats hits inside a cone), CRY (the tooka pulls every hostile within 25 m onto ITSELF for 3 s — the useless thing's one moment of use is being bait it will probably not survive), WRECK (rancor pup slams designated cover through Destruction), RELAY (B1 carries a standing order to a squad outside your own order range), TEND (medical droid works a designated downed body's bleed-out clock), BREACH (wookiee rips a designated cover apart), BOLT / CHARGE / CLIMB (the three mounts). This single slot is what stops twelve kinds being twelve reskins, and the wheel's caption reads the live one, so the wheel says something different for every companion you own. Licensed at rung 2. · **key**: Wheel, straight down.

## The twelve kinds

Its verb is BLOCK: it puts itself between you and the nearest hostile and takes hits inside a cone, so the better it does its job the faster you lose it. Tough enough that you SPEND it rather than nurse it, which makes losing it your fault in a way none of the others are. This is the kill-test animal. · **body**: ONE `CREATURE_PLANS` row (Bodies.js:8420, verified — the row buys skeleton, trunk lathe, dorsal outline, swept tail, belly weak spot, head, per-limb tubes, feet and the `stance` object `_poseWalker` reads) plus ONE archetype row. Four legs, `back: 'scutes'`, `foot: 'paw'`, plate swells at the shoulder. No new code path — `buildBeast` is two lines and is the whole proof. · **mount**: False · **combat**: Controller. `moves: ['grip','lunge','rake']`. High toughness, moderate pace, no ranged answer at all.
- **Tooka kit** — **plays**: IT DOES NOTHING, AND THAT IS THE ENTIRE POINT. Zero attack verbs, the lowest hp in the game, flees anything hostile within 15 m — toward you. The only companion fast enough to follow a sprint, and the most fragile thing you can bring. Its verb is FLUSH, which knocks a body flat instead of killing it — a setup for your blade rather than a substitute for it. Point it at a B2 and you have thrown it away, which is how it teaches the order system. · **body**: CHEAPEST IN THE SET. Shortest time-to-hit in the game, four seconds of survivability in a firefight, will not disengage.

It is also the only one that TALKS, which makes it the pick for a player who wants company rather than a weapon. Its verb is RELAY: send it to a squad and it delivers a standing order to men outside your own order range, which pays part of the distance cost the command system already carries — and in a mode with no army it is a bad gun with a running commentary. It dies to two bolts and it will stand in the open, because that is what a B1 does. · **body**: CHEAPEST DROID BY A WIDE MARGIN. `buildB1` (Bodies.js:5972) verbatim plus one BODY_KITS row. This is the kind to prototype the ranged path on. · **mount**: False · **combat**: Ranged, badly — the B1's own rifle at about a third of its accuracy. No melee verbs.

Slow, heavy, and the only companion whose attacks change the LEVEL rather than the enemy — a scaled slam that shatters the crate a shooter is behind. It TOSSES, reusing the biggest upward impulse in the game at a small scale. And its `scale` reads off `runs`: it gets VISIBLY bigger across its life and its hp and damage do not move by a single point. The body grows and hits nothing harder. That is deliberately placed on the one kind where a player would most expect the trap to have been taken. · **body**: ONE plan row on the shipped `brute` pattern (two legs plus limbs with `role: 'arm'` — a layout `creatureSkeleton` already spans, since the rancor and wampa both use it) at ~0.55 scale, rounder section, shorter tail. No ranged, cannot break contact.

Its verb is TEND, which sends it to a designated downed body to work the bleed-out clock — turning 'somebody is down' from a timer you race into a position you defend. ## Keeping it alive

Half is already built and free. The other half is five things, and one of them is currently a silence.

**FREE TODAY.** Reactions.js selects candidates purely on `o.team !== body.team` and never on `o.trooper` — `findCasualty` (:802), `findPatient` (:1579), the smother branch of `chooseReaction` (:568), `startRally` (:1708). So a companion on your team is already a legal drag target, heal patient, smother beneficiary and rally mate. Your squad will pull your dog out of a fire lane with nothing written. `stepReaction` is driven by `Enemy.update` rather than by `steer`, so the companion also DIVES from a grenade for free. What is missing is only the reverse direction — nothing starts a reaction ON behalf of the companion, because all four `start*` calls live inside `steer`, which we deliberately never enter. Two kinds supply the two that matter (the wookiee drags, the medic heals) from the pack's own tick, and nothing else.

**1. IT IS ALWAYS SLOWER THAN YOU.** Stated under the relationship, repeated here because it is the mechanism and not a flavour note: `pace` is capped at 0.85 of your sprint per kind, and no rung, temper or setting raises it. The check pins the cap.

**2. IT GOES DOWN BEFORE IT DIES,** on `MODES[mode].downed` — a NUMBER already declared on theline (Waves.js:669, verified `downed: 1`), command (:442, `0.6`) and skirmish (:815, `0.6`), and already read by Enemy.js:5661 as a multiple of `DOWN_BLEED`. READ, never written, so Waves.js is untouched and rule 7 holds by construction: a mode that declares no window kills the companion outright, which is the right answer for the dojo and the sandbox. You revive it by standing on it for the same window a trooper gets, and you cannot revive a mount while you are riding it. This is SCOPE.md's own stated mitigation for real-time permadeath — the last word on every death is the player's — reused rather than reinvented, and it is enough. Anything softer and the companion stops costing, which is the one failure this whole layer exists to prevent.

**3. AWAY AND HEEL ARE UNREFUSABLE AT RUNG 0.** The two orders that make a companion safer must never be the ones a fresh companion cannot take. Protection that needs a licence is not protection.

**4. FRIENDLY FIRE IS SCALED, AND IT IS NOT SILENT.** The pack calls `installTeamDamage(e, scale)` directly — it is a free function taking only `(e, scale)` (Command.js:4875, verified) and needs no Trooper, which is why the companion never touches `enlistBody`. That catches blade, bolt, blast, fall and lightning at the one door, and cannot miss a damage kind added later. But it only shouts through `this.commandOf`, which the companion deliberately does not have — so today cutting your own dog in half is COMPLETELY SILENT, and `onFriendlyHit` opens `if (!e.trooper) return;` (verified, Command.js:10523) anyway. The pack emits its own notice, with its own copy, naming the animal by the name you gave it. This is the single most memorable thing this feature can produce and it must not happen without the game saying so.

   Paired defect, handled in the same three statements: `underFire` is WRITTEN by `installTeamDamage` (:4906) and DECAYED only inside `_troops`' walk over `squadsOf(c)` (:11436). A body outside a squad latches it at 3.5 forever. The pack decays it in its own tick — two lines, zero risk, and zero lines in Command.js.

**5. THE FRAGILE KINDS ARE FRAGILE LEGIBLY.** `frag` is a per-kind multiplier on incoming damage from AREA sources only — grenade, quake, blast, fall — and never from aimed fire. A tooka dies to a thermal you did not see; it does not die to the bolt you were supposed to have blocked. That keeps every death explicable, which is SCOPE warning 1's whole requirement: a death the player cannot account for is a death they blame on the AI.

**AND ONE KIND MAKES IT MUTUAL.** The massiff's BLOCK reroutes hits inside a cone onto itself. The better it protects you, the faster you lose it.

## What persists

**A SIXTH KEY, ITS OWN FILE, ITS OWN FOLD DOOR, AND A SURVIVAL TEST WRITTEN AS UNCONDITIONALLY AS keep()'s.**

**THE STORE.** `src/game/Kennel.js` on `makeStore('saber.kennel.v1')` (Store.js:39, verified — an absent key is an empty record, a refused write is mirrored for the life of the page and flips `broken`, and `drop()` clears both so a player's own delete is not undone). Company.js's SHAPE is copied and Company.js is NOT extended, for a structural reason: the roll is keyed by ARMY (`load()` keys on army, `keep()` refuses a foreign manifest at :683) and a companion belongs to the PLAYER.

Record: `{ id, kind, name, look, xp, runs, areas, kills, saves, since, tempers[], story[8], lost: { n, fallen[6] } }`. Everything clamped on the way IN exactly as `readMan` clamps (:366-411) — a hand-edited xp of 5000 is a companion that starts SWORN, and a hand-edited scale is a rancor pup filling the screen. Written through a field whitelist that drops null and undefined and KEEPS `false`, exactly as `save` does (:563). Nothing derived is stored.

**THE CHOICE IS A SETTING; THE ANIMAL IS A RECORD.** Which kind you bring is a settings key with all four of rule 6's obligations. Its identity, rung, tempers and history are not, and the split is load-bearing: `loadSettings` falls back to `{...DEFAULT_SETTINGS}` on any parse failure (Menu.js:2768) and spreads the stored blob over defaults wholesale, so a dead companion living in that blob would be silently RESURRECTED by a corrupt save or a version bump — which is `keep()` reopened by accident.

**ITS OWN FOLD DOOR, AND THIS IS NOT A PREFERENCE.** `keepCompanion(world, stats)` in main.js beside `record()` and `bank()`, called from BOTH `gameOver` (:1904) and `quitToMenu` (:1615), guarded once by `world._compBanked` matching `_banked`/`_recorded`, and gated on `world._companion` rather than on `world.command`. It cannot ride `bank()`: `bank()` returns early on `!d || d.deck || d.versus || session` (main.js:1774), so it never fires in a duel, the dojo, the sandbox, a roguelite with no contingent, the hangar or co-op. The brief says the companion is with you the whole time; that sentence and this guard cannot both be true through `bank()`. `record()`'s call site is the right shape to copy — it fires everywhere, once per world, from both endings.

`leaveHangar` must NOT fold. Walking off the deck is not a run, exactly as it already skips `record()` and `bank()`. `quitToMenu` MUST fold, on the same terms `bank()` is called there with no stats: if quitting is the safe way to keep a companion alive, the withdrawal has been reopened by the back door, and REVIEW-V12.md:114 forbids that in as many words.

**THE DID-IT-GET-OUT TEST IS EXPLICIT, BECAUSE THE DEFAULT IS SILENT IMMORTALITY.** `keep()` strikes off every DEPLOYED name not on the manifest (Company.js:774-785) — but a companion is on no roster, so it is invisible to that rule and would simply never die. That is precisely the defect `company.mjs:430` names about bonds: a mechanism with its price quietly removed and nothing on any screen to say so. So the rule is written out: **it persists if and only if it is alive AND (the run was won OR the pack's own `aboard` flag is set).** Dead, downed at the fold, or standing on the ground when the ramp sealed — gone, live record cleared, one epitaph row kept on the `saneFallen` pattern (:317-338): the name you gave it, the ground, who got it, the minute, the fate. No branch for a run that went badly, because a run that went badly is exactly when a player would want one.

**WHY THE `aboard` FLAG AND NOT THE MANIFEST.** Verified: `Extraction.manifest` is `this._seated.map((b) => b.trooper).filter(Boolean)` (Extraction.js:956), so a companion boards the ship and then does not exist on the list that decides who survived. That one filter is the whole gap between "the companion got on the ship" and "the companion is there next run" — and `keep()` reads exactly that array and may not be reopened. So the manifest is left BYTE-IDENTICAL and the pack keeps its own flag, set when the extraction seats it.

**TWO HAZARDS CARRIED RATHER THAN HIDDEN.**

*(a) THE TWO GUARDS ARE PATH-SCOPED AND A NEW FILE IS LEGAL BY DEFAULT.* `tools/checks/company.mjs:1088/1124` runs the six-word currency scan (points|currency|purchase|upgrade|unlock|buy) on Company.js and Muster.js BY PATH; `session.mjs:560` counts `localStorage.setItem` inside five named files. Kennel.js is invisible to both. COMPANY.md:377-380 already states the rule for this exact class of silence: "That silence is a hazard, not a permission." **Both scans are extended to Kennel.js and Companions.js on the commit that creates them**, or the next person adds a currency and nothing goes red. This is the single most important line in the architecture.

*(b) THERE IS NO ATOMIC MULTI-KEY WRITE.* Store.js writes one JSON object under one key, so a fold touching the roll and the Kennel is two independent `setItem` calls either of which can be refused alone. Mitigation: the companion fold runs FIRST and independently, so a partial failure costs a companion record and never a man; and the Kennel is the only writer of its key, exactly as Company is of its.

**AND A DELETE DOOR, WHICH NOTHING DURABLE HAS TODAY.** `Company.clear`, `Muster.clear` and `clearProgress` are all exported with zero callers anywhere in `src/`. A companion is the first durable record a player will genuinely want to destroy — one they regret naming, one they want to start over with. `Kennel.clear()` ships with a real hold-to-confirm control, not just an export.

**AND THE HONEST LINE IN ALL THREE ROOMS.** `notSaving()` is one sentence on one panel of the Company tab today. The companion lives on the deck and on the field as well, and neither surface reads that flag. The Kennel's broken-store line appears in the menu, on the deck, and once at the fold. Silent data loss is the only unacceptable failure mode in a permadeath game.

## Customisation

This is where the design lights code that has shipped for months and has never been reachable. **`buildQuadruped` accepts `opts.hide`, `opts.plate`, `opts.belly` and `opts.eye` (verified, Bodies.js:8663-8666 — `hideMat(opts.hide ?? P.hide, 0.92)` and three siblings) and NOTHING in the tree has ever passed it anything but the plan's own defaults.** Every creature in the game is wearing its factory colours because there is no door. The companion is the door.

**IT CANNOT BE THE EXISTING DOOR.** `KIT_FIELDS` and `PAINT_FIELDS` have exactly two keys, `flesh` and `steel`, and a creature is neither — `kitOptsFrom` would drop a saved creature colour before it ever reached the builder. Worse, `WEARS` is deliberately PERMISSIVE: a type not in the table gets its kind's whole vocabulary, so a companion routed through it would be offered nine clone armour rows that store a value, light up and change nothing — which is the exact dead control that table was written to prevent.

**SO: `COMPANION_LOOK` in CompanionKinds.js, keyed per KIND**, each row naming only the fields that kind's builder actually reads.

    creatures      hide, plate, belly, eye              (four swatch rows — all four already wired)
    droids         shell, trim, photoreceptor, panels   (DeckCast already ships seven astromech schemes)
    wookiee        pelt, braid + one bandolier chip
    mounts         hide + one blanket chip              (see the cloth note)

Ids stored, never colours, so a re-tuned palette reaches the companions already wearing it. `PAINTS`, `paintById`, `markById` and `cleanCallsign` are reused VERBATIM so there is one palette and one name-cleaner in the tree — and `cleanCallsign` already strips the six characters five screens would otherwise have to escape correctly. One `mark` from the existing `MARKS` table, whose own note (Command.js:841) is literally about "keeping track of companions/pets" and insists COSMETIC is doing real work.

**ITS OWN WRITE DOOR, AND ITS OWN PIN.** `tools/checks/company.mjs:1102-1120` greps the BODY of `export function dress` and pins its fields to exactly `band,callsign,kit,mark,paint`; a sixth goes red. So `dressCompanion(id, look)` is a separate exported function in Kennel.js, and **its own equivalent grep-pin is written on the same commit** — or the cosmetics-only line has a hole in it exactly the width of this feature.

**THE RENDERER IS REUSED WHOLE.** `Menu._dressingHtml`/`_wireDressing` (:7257/:7357) already generates swatch rows, kit chips, an "As issued" default on every row, a quote-free attribute encoding so no value can close an HTML attribute, and one `write({…})` call so the store can never hold a half-written set — plus a real sentence instead of an empty box when a chassis wears nothing. The single refactor it needs is to TAKE its tables as arguments instead of importing the clone ones directly.

**REGION PAINTING IS DELIBERATELY NOT ATTEMPTED IN V1.** `regionDist` (Command.js:1192) measures against a HUMANOID frame — size, limb length, side — and `paintKindOf` routes to two tables plus a fallback, so a hawk, a cat, a pup and a tauntaun would all land on the fallback and paint in the wrong places on all four. Flat paint through the four already-written override slots is honest and is what ships. Per-shape region tables are a later, separate change with their own argument.

**SCARS ARE NOT CHOSEN.** They are what happened to it, applied on the `scorchUp` precedent (Command.js:10466) and unremovable. A companion the player designed is a costume; a companion the player named and the game marked is a history.

**TWO TRAPS TO AVOID BY NAME.** `_trustInTheForce` (Menu.js:5605) walks `.cards, .swatches, .kit-chips` under whatever root it is handed, and `_wireDressing` hands it `document.querySelectorAll('.kit-list')` — a companion block sharing a page with the Jedi wardrobe gets randomised by the wrong button, so its root is chosen deliberately. And the companion page is a THIRD SIBLING in the Company tab's left column, never inside `#company-list`: `company.mjs` censuses `.diff` rows and their `dataset.man` out of that node, which is why the muster slate had to become `#company-muster` (Menu.js:6236 records it), and a companion row there fails seven checks.

## In the hangar

**SETTLED CONTRADICTION: TWO REPRESENTATIONS OF ONE RECORD, NOT ONE BODY IN BOTH ROOMS.**

One of the three designs argued for making the companion an `Enemy` on the deck too, on the grounds that one named body deserves one representation and two would diverge. It is the more beautiful answer and it is refused, for a verified reason: **the hangar World deliberately has no CommandDirector** (World.js:915-937 returns before `this.command` is assigned) because `main.bank()` treats any world with `.command` as a battle and would strike the whole roll on every hangar visit. The deck also has no wave director, no corpse budget and no `pickTarget` context. Putting a live `Enemy` in that room drags `_think`, targeting, the LOD ladder and the death path into a scene built without any of them, in exchange for continuity the player cannot see. The company already solves this the honest way — two representations, one save file — and the Kennel record is the only thing that crosses. Same shape, half the risk.

**THE DECK BODY IS ITS OWN THIRD LIST.** `world._companionDeck`, one figure, built beside `callTheCompany` from the same record the ground body reads, stepped by one `stepCompanion(world, dt)` added to `HangarDirector.update`'s flat ordered step list (verified, Hangar.js:1832-1856 — a list of nine `step*` calls with each ordering constraint written beside it, and the file's own header says it is the only hook the room has that is not a body or a prop). Two lines and an import, in the pattern DeckLife, DeckLift, DeckMirror, DeckFlight and DeckEdit all already use.

It cannot reuse `callTheCompany`: `buildFigure` takes a Muster.lineup trooper record, `poseParade` is a parade-stance solver off a designation, and the gait is attached only when `fig.rig?.get('thighL')` exists (Hangar.js:2131) — a humanoid test with no non-humanoid path behind it. So per kind, off the row and never a branch on a name:

- **droid kinds → `DeckCast.Knockable`** (DeckCast.js:554), which already ships the astromech chassis and its seven panel schemes, its own half-extents and mass on the PROP layer, `drive(x,z,yaw)` to move it while it stands, and the full POST/DOWN/REST/RISE/BACK shove cycle on SHOVE's own clock so a knocked droid lies there exactly as long as a trooper does. Free, and one instance slot in an existing InstancedMesh.
- **wookiee → a Hangar `row`**, because buildFigure + paradeMan + BipedAnimator + mergeFigure + makeShovable all already handle a humanoid.
- **creatures and mounts → a standalone gait stepper in CompanionDeck.js** that reads the same published `built.stance` object `_poseWalker` reads. Two independent producers of that shape already ship (`stanceOf` and `chassisStance`), which is the proof it is a contract and not an accident.

**IT FOLLOWS YOU AND IT SITS.** No parade mark; `row.mark` is recomputed each frame to a point 2.2 m behind you, reusing `stepRowInner`'s existing sidestep-round-the-player (:2296), turn-along-the-pit-kerb (:2315) and climb-the-floor-at-2.4-m/s (:2330). It halts when you halt and it SITS — which is the idle pose that has no home anywhere in the tree today, because `walkPhase` advances at a floor of 0.1 so a standing quadruped cycles its legs forever and the head only tracks a hostile.

**IT IS PUSHABLE AND CUTTABLE ON THE DECK WITH ZERO FILE CHANGES.** `world._deckProps` is a published extension point that `Hangar.deckBladeTargets` (:2441, verified) already reads with an absent-array guard and `sh.down`/`row.aboard` skips, that World.js:4538 already consumes — and that **nothing in `src/` has ever written**; `grep -rn "_deckProps" src/` returns two lines, both readers. Push `{shove}` onto it. The one honest fix: the offered capsule is a fixed man (y+0.15 → y+1.65, r 0.32) whatever the body is, so a hawk at 3 m or a tooka at 0.4 m gets a trooper's hitbox until that reader takes the body's own extent — four lines.

**DRESSED ON THE DECK** through DeckEdit's pipeline unchanged: walk within 6 m, the body breaks ranks 0.55 m toward you and turns, a wheel debounced at 0.22 s, a colour change that runs as a moving vertex-mask wash over 0.8 s instead of a pop, and `leaveDeck` flushing a half-typed name on the frame you walk off. `EDIT_OPS` is asserted equal to the menu's list by `deckedit.mjs`, so the new op and the check edit ship on the same commit.

**THE FOOT-PLANTING DEBT, PAID HERE BECAUSE THE COMPANION MAKES IT UNIGNORABLE.** `_poseWalker` plants feet with `ctx.terrain.height(foot.x, foot.z)` while the hips use `max(this.position.y, gh)` and `_move` places the body with `_groundAt`/`supportHeight`. On a deck, a ramp or a crate the hips ride the surface and the legs reach for the terrain metres below. `Extraction._deckHeight` is the patch already written for one case. The fix is one helper — `groundUnder(world,x,z) = world.floorAt?.(x,z) ?? terrain.height(x,z)` — and one call site; `world.floorAt` (Hangar.js:1307) already knows both pads' kerbs, the parked transport's bay floor and its ramp leaf. **This is a behaviour change for every shipped creature on a ramp, which is a fix and not a regression, and it needs its own check line rather than riding in quietly.**

**BUDGET, and it is why the deck body is per-kind rather than one path:** `MAX_ON_DECK` is 24, 131 PROP-layer bodies already stand there, `stepDeckLife` costs 1.0 ms/frame, the scene is bounded at 320 draws by `hangar.mjs`, and a parade figure costs 15.82 ms to build with 54 meshes and no LOD. One companion is affordable. A merged humanoid with a cape is not free, and a Knockable is nearly free.

And rule 3 holds: no new interior room. It lives in the open bay and on the ramp.

## On the ships

**THE SEAT IS THE ONLY THING THAT MUST BE BUILT, AND GETTING IT WRONG KILLS A NAMED MAN.**

The transport publishes exactly ten slots (Vehicles.js:2423 — 6 bench, 4 standing). `_walkTroops` caps at `room = seats.length` and marks the eleventh body `'left'`, a body marked `left` is off `Extraction.manifest`, and a man off the manifest is executed unconditionally by `Company.keep`. So a companion that takes one of the ten kills one of your men. **Every companion, including the wookiee, rides a RESERVED FLOOR SLOT in the empty bay space between the benches — outside `userData.seats`, never dealt to anybody.** One of the three designs proposed making the wookiee take a real seat as an honest price for being the strongest kind; it is refused. It is a lovely idea and it routes a companion decision into permadeath's ledger, which is the one place this feature must never reach.

**BOARDING FROM THE FIELD IS FREE.** Verified at Extraction.js:1732: `for (const e of w.enemies) { if (e.dead || e.team !== team) continue; … }` — no `trooper` test anywhere. The companion queues by distance with the line, has its `_boardPos` fixed once, latches `_boardRole` to climb, has its velocity zeroed and its wish cleared while `toTarget` is still written so the legs keep cycling, and rides the damped ramp-leaf floor rather than the terrain. `_extracting = 'boarding'` is honoured by `Enemy.update` at :6331, so it stops thinking and stops firing while it queues. Not one line of that is written for this feature.

**THE FLIGHT IS FREE.** `_seat` converts where a body already stands into a slot in the ship's frame with no teleport, for any object with `.position` and optional `.velocity/.grounded/.group/.facing/._syncBody`. `_flyPassengers` walks it at 2.2 m/s writing world position off the hull transform every frame. `_release` puts it off the ramp with `nudgeFromSwing` so it does not land in a lit blade. Player and Enemy both go through it unchanged.

**SEATING AND RELEASE HANG OFF `onPhase`, WHICH HAS NO SUBSCRIBER ANYWHERE.** Verified at Extraction.js:960-965 — `this.onPhase?.(phase, this)` fires from `_enter` for all twelve phases, from `_finish`, and from the `away` branch of `_withdrew`. Seat on `sealing`, release on `unload`, HEEL on every transition, `aboard` flag set on seat. The phase machine is untouched. Two things the handler must tolerate: `onPhase` fires AFTER `this.phase` is set and `this.t` reset, and `done` can arrive with the ship already gone because `_handOffDeparture` nulls `this.group` and `this._model` BEFORE `_finish` runs.

**IT CAN BE STRANDED, AND IT MUST BE.** `_boarding` holds the ramp for stragglers only when `this.withdrawing` (:1657), `LAST_CALL` is 22 s and `_leaveTheRest` (:1687) strands whoever is left by design. The companion joins the straggler wait on exactly a man's terms, capped at LAST_CALL — because a companion that could hold a transport indefinitely is a companion that cannot be lost, and then none of this costs anything.

**THE NO-FLIGHT PATH IS NOT OPTIONAL.** `settings.instantSpawn` makes `beginInsertion` and `begin` both return false and nothing is ever seated (Extraction.js:719/872) — it is what the sandbox, the dojo and EVERY headless check set — and `MODES[mode].insertion === false` turns the flight off for training and sandbox entirely. So the companion has a second arrival route written explicitly: spawned beside the player at deploy, HEEL, no seat, no phase. If that is an afterthought this feature breaks in every check before it breaks in a game.

**DECK SIDE.** `DeckFlight.depart` (:419) gives paths to `c.men` only and `embarkCompany` (:797) walks `c.men` only, so anything else is simply left standing when the hull leaves. The companion gets its own two-leg path and its own reserved bay-floor spot; `takeAboard`/`putAshore` need `row.fig.root`, so the Knockable deck body needs a five-line adapter. And `stepRamp` (:1155) fires `depart()` on the player's 1.1 s dwell alone with no "is anything of mine still on the deck" clause — **it gains a LINE in the countdown, never a block.** "KESS IS STILL CROSSING THE DECK" printed where the countdown goes. A room you cannot leave because a pet is slow is worse than a pet left behind, and the countdown is already the whole interface.

**THE RIG-ROOT DOUBLE-WRITE IS COPIED FROM NEITHER CARRIER.** `Riders.update` (:158-177) and `Extraction._flyPassengers` (:2056-2115) carry byte-identical three-line delta code that has been wrong TWICE in two different ways — first for players (a permanent-identity root with the pelvis written in world space beneath it: hands measured at exactly double the body's position, 0.463 m hand-to-hilt), then for everybody in a seat (pelvises measured at (-519, 4799, -357) against bodies at (-261, 2399, -176)). Extraction's fix is `const rootCarries = false`; Riders still uses the `isLocal === undefined` test. The companion copies neither line and asks the existing carrier instead.

**MOUNTS ARE `Driving.Crew`, NOT `Riders.js`, AND THEY NEED NO NEW BINDING.** `Riders.crew()` is the only door into `bound`, it can only `spawnEnemy` a fresh body from `ARCHETYPES[A.saddle]`, there is no bind for a body that already exists, and nothing in its update suppresses `Player._move` — a Player written to a seat there is walked straight off it next frame. `Driving.Crew` is right and is already the skeleton: both pointers, the seat written from `Enemy.update`'s driven branch (frame step 2) rather than the player's own tick (step 1), because a seat written a step early is 12 cm of rider hanging off the back at speed; throttle and steering off `input.moveAxis`; `Player.damage` rerouted to the mount; four clean exits including the mount dying under you. Three lines change: `whyNotDrive`'s `crewOf(type) > 0` gains `|| A.mount`, `seat()` reads `platform()` instead of `chestY + 0.35·scale off the nose`, and `fire()` refuses outright on `A.mount` — a tauntaun has no trigger. The `drive` binding, its reach, and `HUD._drivePrompt`'s live board prompt with its refusal reason all already exist; a second boarding verb would be the duplicate-table defect this repo keeps deleting. And it refuses on `netMode === 'client'` with a measured note — the seat is not replicated — which a ridden companion inherits exactly, and this design does not pretend otherwise.

## Co-op

**EACH COMMANDER BRINGS ONE. THE RUN DOES NOT WRITE THE KENNEL.**

The settled position splits the difference between the three: field it for everybody, fold it for nobody.

**FIELDED.** One companion per connected player, never two, enforced at the door and not by convention. Which kind is a `Net.SESSION_KEYS` entry, because it is a body on the HOST's field and the host has to build it. The look and the name are `Net.LOCAL_KEYS` beside the rest of LOOK_KEYS, for the reason LOOK_KEYS gives — appearance crosses on the avatar packet, it is not a shared rule. `session.mjs` already fails a key on neither list or on both, so this is enforced rather than remembered.

**NOT FOLDED.** `keepCompanion` returns early in a session. No bond earned, no death recorded, no epitaph, and a client's stored companion is untouched — it neither gains a run nor loses its life. That is the conservative answer and the only one that cannot cause a durable loss the player did not cause.

**AND IT IS SAID BEFORE YOU JOIN, NOT DISCOVERED AFTERWARDS** — on the lobby card, in the same voice and the same place `notSaving()` warns you before you spend an evening naming men into a store that is full. A player who is told is not being cheated.

**WHAT THIS DELIBERATELY DOES NOT ANSWER.** Whose army is on the host's field is the same unanswered question the company already has — `veteransToField` returns null in a session (main.js:201) and `bank()` refuses one (:1774), both on the stated argument that the host's army is the one on the field. That argument is about a ROLL. A companion is not a roll and whose companion it is has never been in doubt, so fielding one decides nothing about the army. Shipping a guess about the army here would settle it silently for the company too, and this design will not do that by accident.

**THE BODY COST IS WHY IT IS ONE AND NEVER TWO.** Four commanders is up to four extra permanently-LOD-0 bodies before a single droid spawns, against a measured ceiling of 40-60 and two player reports of sub-10 fps. Mounts help rather than hurt here — the rider is the player, so a mount is ONE body and not two, which is the other reason the Driving path beats the Riders path. The worst case in this whole design is a four-player session with four wookiees, and the frame check measures exactly that.

**MOUNTS DO NOT WORK OFF-HOST**, inherited and stated: `whyNotDrive` refuses on `netMode === 'client'` (Driving.js:139) with a measured note, and `HUD._drivePrompt` already prints a live refusal reason from the live bindings — so the game says why rather than doing nothing.

## Why it cannot break the game

**THE FOUR UNTOUCHABLES, AND WHY EACH HOLDS BY CONSTRUCTION RATHER THAN BY CARE.**

**THE BLADE.** The pack's `capsules()` returns an empty array, `cut()` returns null, `shatter()` and `damage()` are no-ops — the shape all three shipped packs use, so the blade solver is never offered a contact on the pack itself. The companion BODY is an ordinary Enemy and is in the target list on its own account, which is correct and free: it can be cut, it bleeds the right amount, it loses the right limb. Zero lines in the solver, zero in Cleave, zero new capsule kinds. The only related edit is SEVERANCE rows for two new bone roles, and a throw on an unpriced role is the design working.

**THE FORCE ECONOMY.** No companion order costs Force and nothing it does refunds any. The single contact point is carrying the tooka, which uses the EXISTING grip at the EXISTING price — no new cost row, no POWERS entry (hud-events.mjs counts those slots, so an addition there is a red check and is also the wrong place).

**THE ARMY BRAIN.** Zero lines in Command.js and zero branches in `steer`, because the companion never has a `commandOf` and therefore never enters it. This is the whole payoff of the settled seam choice.

**THE CORPSE BUDGET.** A companion death is one ragdoll, the same one every body already gets. No new corpse class, no retention rule, no exemption.

**IT CAN NEVER BE FIELDED AGAINST YOU.** `companion: true` on the row — a FLAG, not `custom: 'companion'`, which would be a ninth string switched on by name in a file that already switches on eight (Enemy.js ×5, Waves.js ×2, Dojo.js ×2, Presence.js, Vehicles.js) and asserted by name in eight checks; that is rule 7's defect in a different suit. Everything `custom: 'beast'` actually buys is independently expressible as flags that already exist, and the creature companions simply declare `custom: 'beast'` like every other creature, because they ARE beasts. One filter line in the composer, and three assertions make the guarantee real: no level `pool` names a companion key, every row with `companion` has score 0 and threat 0, and `_openTypes` driven over every wave on every shipped level returns none.

**NO MODE IS EVER TESTED BY NAME.** The bleed-out window is `MODES[mode].downed`, a number already on three mode rows and already read in Enemy.js. Whether the flight runs is `MODES[].insertion`. Whether a mount can be boarded is `A.mount`. Nowhere in this design does a mode string appear.

**THE ARCHETYPE IS CLONED BEFORE ANY WRITE.** ARCHETYPES rows are shared per type; `enlistBody` clones by identity and `installFlight` clones unconditionally (verified, Flight.js:447 with a note saying writing A.float on the table would fly every Geonosian in lockstep), both because attributes.mjs caught the compounding bug. The pack clones on the same line and never writes ARCHETYPES itself.

**NO NEW DRAW FROM `enemyRng`.** `_build` takes seeded draws for walkPhase, hoverPhase and the form roll from the module-level stream, and `_auditT` is deliberately taken OFF it with a note saying one extra `rng()` call per body constructed shifts every composed wave, form roll and gait phase in the game. Companion randomness gets its own stream seeded off the record id, and the check asserts it.

**NO COMPANION WEARS CLOTH.** Engine.js sizes QUALITY.cloth on "every enemy wearing exactly one cape" and cloth-cost.mjs holds every archetype to `g.n === 1`. A saddle blanket, a banner and a bandolier ship as geometry, not simulation, until somebody measures the alternative.

**AND TURNING IT OFF COSTS NOTHING.** No companion chosen → `attachCompanions` is never called, no pack exists, no prop ticks, nothing spawns, no HUD node is built, no store is read. Cost at rest is zero — the same property that lets `attachRiders` sit in the tree with one caller.

## The checks

- THE LADDER CARRIES NO NUMBERS AT ALL. Every COMPANION_RANKS row is asserted to contain no hp, dmg, speed, pace, armour or any numeric multiplier FIELD — the absence of the field, not a value of 1.00, because a field at 1.00 is an invitation. Deliberately stricter than command.mjs:833's monotonicity clause, and the design says why: twenty-four men average out and one companion does not.
- TOP RUNG REACHABLE IN ONE RUN, AND NOT BEFORE 40% OF IT. Copied verbatim from command.mjs:845-848 and DRIVEN through a real five-area campaign's deed count rather than transcribed. This is the clause that proves persistence is a shortcut to a ceiling and never a new one.
- PACE IS CAPPED AND CANNOT BE RAISED. Every kind's pace is under 0.85 of the player's sprint, and no rung, temper or setting path can move it. This is the 'less mobile than you' mechanism and it must not be tuned away by somebody trying to make the companion feel better.
- EVERY DUTY NAMES A CONSUMER THAT EXISTS, on licence.mjs's shape, driven against a real pack and a real body. A rung whose licence sentence promises something no code reads fails — the refusal sentences are printed on a page and read as a promise.
- EVERY TEMPER IS TWO-SIDED, PRICES NET <= 0, AND SHEDS. priceTemper over the whole table, with every `sheds` predicate driven to a real true AND a real false so a temporary temper is proven to refund its swing. No permanent temper priced below the tax floor.
- TWELVE KINDS, TWELVE BEHAVIOURS. Every KINDS row declares a distinct kind verb and a distinct combat profile, and no two rows differ only in numbers. This is what makes 'twelve reskins is a failure' mechanical instead of a hope, and it is the check that fails first if the schedule starts cutting corners.
- A COMPANION IS NEVER IN A POOL. No level pool names a companion key; every row with `companion` has score 0 and threat 0; _openTypes and _setPiece driven over every wave on every shipped level return none.
- EXACTLY ONE COMPANION PER COMMANDER, ALWAYS. No rung, temper, record field or setting can produce a second. A companion that adds a body to the line is company.mjs's 'rank, not headcount' defect with fur on it.
- SEVERANCE PRICES EVERY BONE ON ALL TWELVE BODIES. Build each one, walk every bone's role through severanceOf. Both Bone's constructor and severance() throw on an unpriced role, and this check is what makes the throw arrive in CI instead of under a blade.
- THE PACK IS NEVER A BLADE TARGET. capsules() empty, cut() null, damage() false, and the pack absent from bladeTargets' output on a frame with a live companion and a lit blade. Plus the Levy lesson pinned: pack.body.position is a real Vector3, because _resolveBlades calls distanceToSquared on every prop before it looks at anything.
- PERSISTENCE IS EARNED, NOT DEFAULT. keepCompanion driven through four endings — won, withdrew-aboard, withdrew-and-left-behind, quit mid-run — asserting the last two lose it and write one epitaph. The failure this catches is silent immortality: a companion is on no roster, so keep()'s strike-off rule cannot see it and the default behaviour is that it never dies.
- THE FOLD FIRES IN EVERY MODE, EXACTLY ONCE PER WORLD. gameOver and quitToMenu driven across all eleven modes. This is what proves the design did not quietly inherit bank()'s `!d || d.deck || d.versus || session` guard. And leaveHangar is asserted NOT to fold.
- NO SEAT IS TAKEN FROM A MAN. Board ten troopers and a companion through the real ExtractionDirector and assert manifest.length === 10 and Extraction.manifest is byte-identical in shape. Under keep(), a man off the manifest is dead.
- THE STORE SCANS NOW SEE THE NEW FILES. company.mjs's six-word currency grep covers Kennel.js and Companions.js by path; session.mjs's durable-writer census counts six named files, not five; dressCompanion's field list is grep-pinned the way dress's is. The check that guards the guards, shipped on the same commit as the store.
- THE INPUT TABLE STILL AGREES WITH ITSELF, AND THE FRAME BUDGET HOLDS. conflicts(defaultBindings()) === [] with the companion row on; the row is on exactly one Net list; controls.mjs's freeCodes()/spares() fixtures still find their unbound codes. And one companion costs no more than 1.5 ms on the Geonosis wave the player reported sub-10 fps on, with the 40-60 body ceiling loaded, measured with and without — a number with a band, on the measured worst case, plus the four-player co-op case. Also pins the two live defects fixed here (built.proxy.y honoured every frame, feet and hips agreeing on a deck) so neither can come back.

## The kill test

**ONE KIND, ONE ORDER, ONE AFTERNOON. ABOUT 300 LINES ACROSS THREE FILES. AND IT MUST BE ABLE TO SAY NO.**

**BUILD:** the massiff, in Skirmish on Geonosis — a mode with NO CommandDirector, chosen deliberately, because that is the case every other design would have failed. One CREATURE_PLANS row, one archetype row with `companion: true`, a `spawnEnemy` at deploy, `installTeamDamage` called directly, `installCompanionAim` (the Levy `_think` wrap answering from `world._hostilesFor`), `installCompanionMove` writing a wish toward a point 2.2 m behind the player, and the one binding whose TAP issues HEEL or SEEK off the reticle. No store, no Kennel, no rungs, no tempers, no wheel, no HUD, no deck, no ships, no customisation, no second kind.

The massiff because one plan row buys the skeleton, trunk, outline, head, feet, belly weak spot, stance, gait, move set with pose curves, dismemberment pricing, three LOD rungs, a cohort, topple and a voice — so almost nothing is being measured except the five things that decide whether the architecture is right.

**FIVE MEASUREMENTS, WITH NUMBERS AND WITH WHAT EACH FAILURE KILLS.**

**1. IT FINDS ENEMIES IN A MODE WITH NO ARMY.** Over 60 s of Skirmish, the companion engages hostiles and never once stands with `wish === null` while a hostile is inside 20 m. *Fails → the Levy seam is wrong and the whole "yours in every mode" claim collapses. This is the first thing to check because it is the claim everything else rests on, and it is the one the original hazard list said was the worst in the survey.*

**2. IT FOLLOWS WITHOUT BEING A NUISANCE.** Over 120 s of normal play across crates, a trench and a ramp: inside 6 m of the player for ≥85% of frames, and never inside 1.2 m for more than 0.5 s continuously. The second half is the one that matters — it must not stand in you. *Fails → the `_move`-wrapper seam is the wrong seam, and that is a foundation, not a tuning number.*

**3. IT SURVIVES BEING A FRIENDLY.** Zero same-team damage events from its own attacks over 120 s — `_beastBrain.hitTarget` bills `t.damage?.(...)` against whatever the pick handed it with no team test of any kind, so this is a real risk and not a formality. And cut it with your own blade: the hit is scaled and the game says something, by name. And `findPatient` returns it when it is hurt and a medic is near, which proves the free half of the protection loop is actually free. *Fails → the team model is wrong at the foundation.*

**4. FRAME TIME IS WITHIN 1.5 ms.** Measured on the Geonosis wave the player reported sub-10 fps on, with 40-60 bodies loaded. A companion is permanently inside 30 m and therefore permanently LOD 0: `ANIM_STEP[0]` is 0 so it is a full skeletal solve every frame, every decoration mesh drawn, cloth on, a Presence voice slot, never a merged skin and never a cohort. *Fails → the kind list shrinks and the expensive kinds do not ship. It does NOT mean `_poseWalker` grows a companion special case; an alternate-frame IK rung is a change to the animation path every body depends on and must be its own commit with its own argument and its own check.*

**5. IT DIES AND THE RUN IS MEASURABLY WORSE.** Play the same wave twice, once with it and once after losing it, and you must be able to SAY what you lost — a flank no longer covered, a body that was no longer between you and the thing that reached you. And you must be annoyed, and able to name what you did wrong. *Fails → the companion is a cosmetic, and no amount of persistence, growth or customisation will fix it, because a thing that costs nothing to lose is a thing nobody protects. Make it more fragile and re-run ONCE. If it still does not need you, stop building.*

**WHY HEEL AND NOT "KILL THAT ONE":** a pet that fights is a solved and unremarkable thing. HEEL is the relationship — the player choosing to protect something at a cost to their own tempo, which is the load-bearing claim of the entire brief.

**WHAT THE TEST DELIBERATELY DOES NOT ASK:** whether persistence feels good. That cannot be answered in a day and it is not the risk — the store is the best-understood part of this design and Company.js is a worked example sitting right there. All the risk is in the minute-to-minute.

**THE TRANCHES, so the schedule cuts from the right end.** T1: massiff, tuk, tooka, B1 — four kinds, two data rows each, plus the pack, the store, the wheel and the licence ladder. T2: the three mounts — one gate line in `_measurePlatform` and three Driving lines. T3: pup, wookiee, hawk. T4: astromech, medical droid — the two with real builder cost and the two that need SEVERANCE and Presence rows. Measurement 4 is the gate on T3 and T4. Shipping four companions that genuinely play differently beats twelve where eight are rescaled quadrupeds and the last four are half-finished.

## The honest doubt

**THE FRAME BUDGET, AND I HAVE NOT SOLVED IT — I HAVE ONLY ARRANGED TO FIND OUT EARLY.**

Every rung of the LOD ladder keys on camera distance: `_applyLod`'s band selection, the merged skin at 62 m, the cohort past L3_AT, `clothOn` against a default clothCut of 30, and Presence's nearest-N voice budget. A companion is inside 30 m of somebody forever. So it is permanently LOD 0 — full skeletal solve every frame, every decoration mesh drawn, cloth on, a voice slot held, never a merged skin, never a cohort. The measured drop the ladder buys is real (1064 draws → 194 → 27 on 42 bodies) and the companion gets none of it, ever. `_poseWalker`'s per-limb IK loop is also unbounded in leg count, and the limb loop is skipped past LOD 1 — right for a horde, useless for the one body that is never far away. Against a measured ceiling of 40-60 live bodies and two player reports of sub-10 fps, one companion is not one average body's worth of cost; it is one worst-case body's worth, permanently, in exactly the frames that are already the worst ones, plus a nameplate node and a pack pass.

The mitigation I would actually want is a non-distance rung — a proximity-locked body that is neither attacking nor under the reticle solving its IK on alternate frames. That is a new axis in a ladder that has only ever had one, it touches the animation path every body depends on, and it is the piece I am least confident is safe. So it is explicitly **not** in this design. Measurement 4 of the kill test is the gate, and if it fails the answer is that the kind list shrinks — starting with the wookiee, which is a full-detail humanoid and the most expensive single thing here. The astromech publishing a zero-limb stance is the one genuinely cheap body in the set and is the hedge.

**SECOND: TWELVE IS TOO MANY FOR THE BODY WORK, AND THE TRANCHES ARE ME SAYING SO IN THE SCHEDULE RATHER THAN IN THE PROSE.** CREATURE_PLANS genuinely buys four of them for one data row each and `buildBeast` being two lines is the proof. The other eight are not. There is no DROID_PLANS — every droid in the tree is a bespoke builder and the two non-rigged ones fall out of the rig path entirely into `built.group` with their own pose function and their own brain — so the astromech and the medical droid are builders, not rows. The hawk needs a wing role in a vocabulary that expands limbs left+right and a fifth head branch. The mounts need a saddle path that does not exist and run straight into `A.big` being load-bearing in four unrelated places. If it comes to it, five kinds that each genuinely play differently — massiff, tuk, astromech, tooka, tauntaun — cover every pattern the twelve express (block, hunt, tool, liability, mount) and the rest are content added one row at a time later.

**THIRD, SMALL, REAL, AND NOBODY ASKED FOR IT.** `_friendNear` (Enemy.js:6218) counts anything with `o.team === e.team` and has no trooper test, so a companion standing beside a shaken trooper already suppresses the 'alone' refusal and the MORALE.ALONE drift — for free, in every mode, forever. That is a genuine buff to the army arriving inside a feature that is supposed to be separate from it, and nothing budgets for it. The honest fix is one line inside `_friendNear`, which is in the army brain and on the do-not-touch list for good reasons. So: measure it. Run the advance-order landing rate with and without a companion in the line. If it moves, take that one line to review as its own change with its own argument, rather than shipping a quiet buff hidden inside a pet.

**FOURTH, AND THE ONE I CANNOT TEST FROM HERE: whether a licence ladder actually FEELS like growth.** "It does more of what I ask, further from me, for longer" is the law-clean answer and I believe it is the right one, but it is subtler than a bar filling and the whole design rests on it landing. The mitigation is that the wheel prints the refusal sentence on every cold slot, so the player sees the thing they cannot yet order every time they open it, and rung 1 arrives fast. If it still reads as nothing happening, the honest fix is not to add numbers — it is to make the rungs arrive sooner and the refusals louder.
