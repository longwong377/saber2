*Logged from the player's list, 2026-09-04. Same treatment as `V15.md`: the

## 0. THE THESIS, WHICH THE LIST IS ALREADY ABOUT

The player closed with:

> *"dont stop here im only human you must fucking see where I'm going with all
> of this like don't just take my word for it go ahead and think of and make
> even more parts of the ship in the same vein as what I have asked for; you
> must see a method to this madness."*

I do, and it is one sentence:

> **Every function that lives on a menu becomes a place with a person in it,
> until the menu has nothing left to hold.**

That is the through-line of all fourteen items. Choosing a mission is the
command deck. Editing a saber is the armoury. Training is a holodeck. Buying
things is a shop with a shopkeeper who is not there tomorrow. Getting hurt is
a medbay. Getting paid is credits. Doing nothing is a bar. And it is said
outright twice in the list — *"eventually I want to explore the idea of
removing the functionality from the main menu to make the station more
immersive"*, and *"these will eventually be removed from the main menu too"*.

**SHARK built the station as a PLACE. V15 made it yours. V16 makes it the
game.** The menu's job shrinks to: new game, continue, settings, quit.

### And the second thesis, which is what makes it affordable

Fourteen features is a year. **Four systems is a season**, and every item on
the list is one of four:

| System | What it is | The items it carries |
|---|---|---|
| **THE COUNTER** | a person, a stock list that rerolls, a price, a faction gate | shops, food, armoury, saber editing, black market |
| **THE CONSOLE** | a fixed thing in a room that opens a panel the menu used to hold | mode select, holodeck, stratagems, companion screen |
| **THE ROSTER** | station people who are not scenery — they remember, they ask, they leave | quests, troops on leave, casino opponents, vendors |
| **THE SPECTACLE** | a seeded simulation you can research, bet on, and watch | podracing, gladiator pits, The Pit (#18) |

Write four systems well and the fourteen features are content in tables.
Write fourteen features and the fourth one is unmaintainable.

## 1. MOST OF IT IS ALREADY A ROOM

The single most important fact about this list: **`SHARK.md`'s gazetteer
already has the buildings.** 55 places exist, are built, are walkable, are
lit, and are measured. V16 is very largely **making rooms that exist do the
thing their sign already promises**:

| Asked for | Already exists | Its verb today |
|---|---|---|
| bars | `#14 Cantina "The Long Night"` | *sit and drink* |
| betting | **`#18 The Pit`** | ***watch and bet*** |
| gladiator matches | `#20 The Arena` | *fight a bout* |
| watch the races | `#19 Holo-theatre` | *watch your last run* |
| food, markets, stalls | `#15 The Fresh Air`, `#16 Galley`, `#17 Food court` | *eat* |
| the medbay doing something | `#43 Medbay`, `#44 Bacta ward`, `#45 Morgue` | *see your wounded* |
| saber editing at the armoury | `#46 Armoury` (kiosk `loadout`) | *draw a loadout* |
| mission select at the command deck | `#41 Command / CIC` (kiosk `campaign`) | *brief the next campaign* |
| stratagems at engineering | `#48 Reactor hall`, `#50 Fabrication` | *walk the catwalks* |
| shops | `#9 The Concourse` + four kiosk rooms | *browse* |
| co-op apartments | `#31` Human residential, `#38` hostel | *walk* |

**Genuinely new places needed: three.** A casino/nightclub, a holodeck, and a
second bar at the other end of the class ladder. Everything else is a fitting-
out job on a room that is already standing, which is an enormous saving and
is why this list is possible at all.

## 2. THE LANES

### LANE A — THE STATION IS THE MENU

#### A1. The command deck picks the mission, and the station flies there

> *"maybe the command deck or somewhere like that … if youre at a different
> map/planet already and you choose a different map you have to fly there
> before starting a game at that planet … there's this cool flying animation
> where you actually see entire station (should be the actual real detailed
> model) warp to that location (should take maybe 5-10 seconds and look really
> cool) there should absolutely not be a loading screen this is just for
> immersion sake … now obviously as you can see in the command deck's
> expansive windows that you are now orbiting whatever planet you choose."*

This is the keystone item and it is already half-built:

- `#41 Command / CIC` already carries `kiosk: 'campaign'`.
- The station already knows what is outside — V15 wired `configureOrbit` and
  `dressDeckBattle` off `outsideLevel(world)`, so the windows already show a
  real theatre and changing `_pickedLevel` changes what is out there.
- The lift already crosses between worlds without a loading plate
  (`captureStill`, V15 §1.5).

So the warp is **not a level load**. It is: change `_pickedLevel`, play a
transition on the sky, re-configure the orbit, done. The world is never
rebuilt, which is exactly why *"there should absolutely not be a loading
screen"* is achievable rather than aspirational.

**The five seconds, staged.** Not one effect — a sequence, because a sequence
reads as a procedure and a procedure reads as a ship:
1. **The order.** You give it at the plot table. A watch officer repeats it
   back. (Uses the `#41` room and its people.)
2. **The call.** Klaxon, the deck lighting steps to transit amber, the crowd
   noise in the drum drops. Every deck hears it, not just the one you are on.
3. **The turn.** The starfield rotates as the station comes onto its bearing.
   This is a uniform on the dome, not a camera move — the player keeps
   control the entire time, which is the whole difference between immersion
   and a cutscene.
4. **The jump.** Star-lines, 1.5 s. The fleet action outside is torn down.
5. **The arrival.** New planet swells in, orbit reconfigured, fleet action for
   the new theatre dressed, lighting steps back, PA says where you are.

**And you see the station from outside.** The player asked for *"the actual
real detailed model"*. `#55 The station's outside` is already a gazetteer row
and SHARK §4 already wanted a Starfury flying round it. The warp is the second
reason to build that exterior, so the two share it. Cutting away from the
player to an exterior camera contradicts "not a cutscene", so: the exterior
view is what you see **from the observation dome (#54) and the command deck's
own windows** — you watch the hull and the drum against the star-lines. If a
player wants the beauty shot they go up to the dome first. That is better than
taking the camera off them, and it gives `#54` a job.

**Menu removal is staged, not sudden.** The command deck gains mission select;
the menu keeps it until the station path is proven; then the menu's entry
becomes "go to the station".

#### A2. The holodeck — training and sandbox, entered as a room

> *"there will be a holodeck training area/dogo area on the ship … you walk/
> find the dojo/holodeck (name it something unique) and there at the console
> you just get the regular training/sanbox menus and enter the holodeck
> equivalent after selecting whatever map."*

A new place. Name: **#57 The Repeating Room** — a Minbari-built training hall
the station inherited; the species already lives here (`#34`) and a name that
is a description rather than a brand fits the rest of the gazetteer (*The
Standing*, *The Long Night*, *The Fresh Air*).

Mechanically the cheapest item on the entire list: it is `#41`'s console
pointed at the training and sandbox modes instead of the campaign. The room's
value is the **dressing** — a deck that is bare grid when idle and becomes the
chosen map's ground when you commit, which is one material swap and reads as
magic.

Deck 40, `outer`, near `#21 Gym` and `#20 The Arena` so the physical part of
the station is in one quarter.

#### A3. Engineering upgrades stratagems

> *"there's a certain part of the ship where you can customize and upgrade
> your strategems maybe engineering … if you use certain strategems certain
> number of times maybe you can upgrade them and unlock new ones, come up with
> a minigame here."*

`#48 Reactor hall` and `#50 Fabrication` both exist. Stratagems are ship-to-
ground fire, so the honest room is **`#42 Comms & sensor room`** — that is
where a fire mission is called from — with `#50 Fabrication` for the hardware.
Split: **you upgrade a stratagem at Fabrication (you make the thing) and you
re-tune its call at Comms.**

**Usage-count unlocks are a cross-run progression and collide with the
doctrine in §4 below.** The resolution: usage counts unlock *variants*, never
raw power — a longer fuse, a tighter pattern, a different shell — and the
variant is a sidegrade the player chooses per run. That satisfies *"upgrade
them and unlock new ones"* without becoming a power ladder.

**The minigame**: calibration. A firing solution is three sliders against a
drifting target — spread, delay, bearing — and how well you land it sets the
variant's tuning for the next run only. Skill, repeatable, no stored number.

#### A4. The armoury owns the lightsaber

> *"maybe you can only adjust stuff with your lightsaber at the armory? …
> that's the only place where you can edit your lightsaber … the shopseller
> should be someone who would know stuff about lightsabers maybe a
> mandalorian."*

`#46 Armoury` exists with `kiosk: 'loadout'`. `#10 The Forge` already exists
on deck 40 with `kiosk: 'hilt'` and the verb *"work the hilt and blade"* —
which is already exactly this feature, one deck down. **So the answer is not
to move it to the armoury; it is to make `#10 The Forge` the only door and
give it the Mandalorian.**

Then `#46 Armoury` keeps the *loadout* (what you carry) and gains the range,
and the two rooms have one job each instead of a shared one. A Mandalorian
armourer at the Forge is right on every axis — beskar, the Forge's name, and
a species who would sell to either side.

Yes to *"you can only edit your lightsaber here"*, staged the same way as A1:
the menu's saber tab stays until the room is proven.

### LANE B — THE COUNTER (money, stock, and what is on the shelf today)

#### B1. Credits

> *"very very long list of items you can purchase with credits that you get
> for doing differnt stuff in the game and playing the game."*

One currency, earned by playing, spent at counters. **See §4 — this is the one
item on the list that collides with a written law of the codebase, and it has
to be settled before a line is written.**

#### B2. The shelf rerolls, and that is the whole feature

> *"the shops don't always have the same things."*

This is the most important sentence in the shop section and the cheapest thing
in it. A fixed catalogue is a wiki page; a shelf that rerolls is a reason to
walk to the Zocalo. One mechanism, shared by every counter in the station:

- Every counter has a **stock table** (everything it could ever sell) and a
  **shelf** (six to ten rows drawn from it).
- The shelf is drawn from a seed of `(counter id, the day)`, so it is the same
  for everyone on the station on that day and it changes when the clock rolls
  over. The station clock already exists and already persists (`StationSave`).
- Rarer rows are rarer draws, and price rises with tier — *"they have to get
  cooler and cooler and cooler the more expensive they get"* is a rarity
  ladder with the art budget spent at the top.
- A row you can afford but did not buy is gone tomorrow. That is the hook.

#### B3. Two kinds of thing, and only two

> *"maybe cosmetic stuff you buy is permanent and you can also buy powerups
> similar to the stuff you can get in the holocrons but they are temporary and
> do not persist when you die."*

The player has drawn the exact line the codebase needs (see §4):

- **KEEPSAKES** — cosmetic, permanent. Paint, hilt parts, robes, apartment
  furniture, companion collars and marks, casino chips, trophies. Change how
  things look and nothing else. Bought once, yours forever.
- **PROVISIONS** — a run's worth of something, gone when the run ends. Food
  buffs, a temporary Holocron facet, a stim, a stratagem charge. **Explicitly
  do not survive death**, in the player's own words.

Nothing else is sellable. A third category — permanent power — is the thing
`Progress.js` forbids and the player did not ask for.

#### B4. Faction gates

> *"certain items are asscessible only to jedi and some only to sith
> affiliated, like maybe some vendors reduce to work with sepratists while the
> black market smuggler types only deal with sith."*

`world._deckFaction` and `Databank.factionOf` already exist and the station
already reads them. A stock row carries an optional `side`, and a counter
carries an optional `refuse` — the vendor says why, in one line, in character.
This also gives V15's `standing` (already in `StationSave`, already falls when
you hurt a resident) its first real reader: **a vendor who has heard about you
charges more, or shuts the shutter.**

The black market is a place: **#58 The Underlift** — three shipping
containers welded into the ring's service gap on deck 44, behind the laundry.
Open only some days. Sith/Separatist stock, no receipts.

#### B5. Food, and eating before a fight

> *"maybe you have to actually buy food at the market to keep yourself fed,
> you should ideally have a full stomach before battle, your companion needs
> to be fed too … different types of food cost money and there's not always
> the same food available, you actually see them cook … certain types of food
> even give you certain buffs that last for a limited amount of time … you can
> even buy food and take it to your apartment and save it for later … Droids
> instead of being fed are 'fed' in their own way by being charged."*

Food is a PROVISION with three properties: it is bought at a counter, it can
be **carried and stored at home** (which is the apartment's first real
function beyond decoration), and it is **eaten for a timed buff**.

**On hunger as a meter: no.** *"you should ideally have a full stomach before
battle"* — "ideally" is the word, and a hunger bar that punishes you for not
shopping turns a pleasure into a chore and turns every run into a trip to the
shops first. **Eating is upside only.** Full stomach = a buff. Empty stomach =
no buff, and nothing else. The player gets the ritual without the leash.

The rooms exist: `#15 The Fresh Air` (sit-down), `#17 Food court` (cheap and
many), `#16 Galley` (the crew's, free, plain — the one that costs nothing and
buffs least), plus new stalls in `#9 The Concourse` and one in the `#32 Narn
quarter` and `#36 methane quarter` that sell things a human should not eat.

**You watch them cook.** One animation loop per stall, keyed to the dish. This
is the cheapest atmosphere in the whole list and the most visible.

**Droids charge.** A companion droid takes a power cell instead of a meal, at
`#51 Droid pool` or from a wall socket at home. Same system, different noun,
and it lets `#51` do something.

### LANE C — THE ROSTER (the people are the content)

#### C1. The medbay

> *"lets make the medbay area of the station actually do something … when you
> die you wake up in the med bay; also like maybe your troops/companions when
> they survive with really low health they are taken to the medbay … maybe
> your injured troops have to actually follow you to the medbay to get healed
> after a run like you get off the transport and you give the follow me order
> to the injured … you actually see them being healed (would take time in
> game) … maybe the healthier troops carry the injured troops on a floating
> stretcher."*

This is the best item on the list because it makes a **consequence visible**
that the game already tracks and never shows. `Company.js` already keeps the
roll, the wounded and the fallen; `#43 Medbay`, `#44 Bacta ward` and `#45
Morgue & memorial` are already built rooms.

The loop:
1. **You come back with wounded.** They are on the roll already.
2. **They walk off the transport with you** — the existing follow order,
   pointed at the medbay. Walking wounded walk; the badly hurt go on a
   repulsor stretcher carried by two who are not. (`Companions.js` already has
   carry and leash behaviour to draw on.)
3. **They check in.** A medical droid takes each one; you see them go into a
   bacta tank in `#44`. The tank is occupied while they heal.
4. **Healing takes station hours, not runs.** The clock already runs at one
   game hour per two real minutes and already persists. A man is out for six
   hours; go and do something else, come back, he is on his feet. That is a
   *reason for the clock to exist*, which it currently lacks.
5. **You can leave them.** Nothing forces the walk. A man not checked in heals
   slower. Consequence, not chore.
6. **You wake here.** Death puts you on a bed in `#43` with the roll beside
   you, which is a far better death screen than a death screen.

Companions the same, at `#43` or the habitat, whichever you take them to.

#### C2. Bars, leave, and morale

> *"add one or two bars and a casino/nightclub … imagine certain things being
> really fancy and incredibly upscale and others being incredibly grimy and
> sleezy … you can assign troops to go on leave and they will get increased
> morale and will heal over time, you will actually see your real troops
> relaxing there and gambling/drinking/talking to women/escorts/dancers etc."*

Two bars at opposite ends and they should not be subtle about it:
- **`#14 The Long Night`** (exists) — the grimy one. Dock workers, spacers,
  bad light, a card game in the corner that never ends.
- **`#59 The Ascendant`** — new, deck 48 outer, near command. Upscale to the
  point of coldness. Officers, diplomats, a Minbari string instrument, a view.

**Leave is the mechanic and it pairs with the medbay.** You assign men from
the roll to leave; they walk there; they are visibly in the bar for the hours
they are off; morale rises and light wounds close. A man on leave is not
available for the next run. That is a real decision with a real cost, and it
uses the roster the game already keeps.

**And they are your actual men.** `StationLife` already spawns real bodies
with real archetypes and `Company.js` already stores the roll — the same
figure that fought beside you is the one at the bar, with his own name over
him if you look.

#### C3. Quests from people who are not always there

> *"you can talk to npcs there so maybe give you certain quests (very long
> list of potential ones and totally random and not always there and the npcs
> aren't always in the same place either so it's a chance thing) … maybe the
> npcs give you certain quests like kill X number of X or make sure that a
> specific trooper survives a run and returned home … when you complete a
> certain quest it is recorded and you go back to that npc who will be there
> since you compelted the quest."*

The rule the player states matters more than the quests: **the giver is a
chance encounter, but the payer is guaranteed.** Take a job and that NPC is
pinned to the station until you come back for the money. Everyone else
rerolls.

Quest shapes drawn from what the game already counts (no new telemetry):
- **a number** — kill N of a type, in one run
- **a name** — bring a specific man home alive (uses `Company`)
- **a place** — reach an area, or hold one
- **a manner** — with the blade only; without the Force; no man lost
- **a thing** — recover something, which is `#25 Lost & found`'s only reason
  to exist today
- **a mercy** — do not kill a particular kind, all run

Rewards are credits and KEEPSAKES, never power.

#### C4. The faces reroll

> *"the same shop owner doesnt always look the same like between runs or maybe
> deaths idk everything other than your apartment/companion should be
> refreshed/randomized right because otherwise it would get stale seeing the
> same people always doing the same things."*

Correct and already nearly free: `StationCast.resident(seed)` builds a
resident from a seed across all fifteen species. Seed the counters and the
quest-givers from `(role, the day)` and the station's population turns over
without a line of new content. **Three things are pinned and never reroll:**
your home, your companion, and anyone who owes you money.

### LANE D — THE SPECTACLE (one engine, three skins)

> *"you could also be able to bet on sports games but obviously these should
> be in lore or sci games so imagine if you could bet on podraces … these will
> be real races without pre-determined outcomes … this argorithm that takes
> into account the podracer, the weather, the pilot, the podracer's hx, the
> map, their competitors … there will be an element of randomness … a good
> better will probably make money over time … so even the game doesn't know
> who will race until the victor is called … maybe the scnes you sometimes see
> play out on the screens are actually real scenes from that podrace you're
> watching."*
>
> *"the second sport … gladitorial/droid combat. Pitting sentients, beasts, or
> customized combat droids against each other in death matches with massive
> underground syndicates taking bets."*

**These are one system.** A spectacle is:

1. **A CARD** — a field of entrants, each with attributes and a history.
2. **A GROUND** — a track or an arena, with conditions.
3. **A SIM** — a tick loop with genuine randomness that nobody, including the
   game, has run before the player places the bet.
4. **A FORM BOOK** — everything above, readable, so research pays.
5. **A WINDOW** — the sim rendered as moments on a screen in a room.

The player's *"even the game doesn't know who will race until the victor is
called"* is a hard requirement and a good one: **the sim must run forward from
the bet, never be drawn and then narrated backwards.** That is also the
cheapest honest implementation, and the only one where researching form can
actually pay.

**Why a good bettor should win over time.** Odds are set by the house from the
*public* form (recent results, a headline rating). The sim uses *hidden* terms
the odds do not price — a pilot's temperament on a wet track, a droid's
overheat threshold, a grudge from the last meeting. Research is what turns a
hidden term into a known one. That is exactly how real form works and it makes
the reading room worth the walk.

**Three skins on one engine:**
- **Podracing** — `#19 Holo-theatre` (exists, *"watch your last run"*) shows
  the feed; 2–3 tracks; weather; the field of racers persists across days and
  builds a real history you can study.
- **Gladiator / droid combat** — **`#18 The Pit`**, whose verb in the
  gazetteer is *already* *"watch and bet"*. Entrants are sentients, beasts and
  custom droids, which means **every body builder the game already has** is a
  potential entrant. The player's own beasts and droids could enter.
- **`#20 The Arena`** — the one where *you* are the entrant. Already exists,
  already says *"fight a bout"*. Same card, same odds, and people bet on you.

**Rendering the moments** is the part that could sink this. It is not video.
It is: the sim emits events (an overtake at gate 4, a wall strike, a lead
change), and the screen shows a short procedurally posed shot of *that* event
with the *actual* entrants in it. Reuse of the existing body and vehicle
builders, plus the replay camera the Holo-theatre already implies.

#### D1. Playable casino games

> *"you should be able to play some of the casino games these should be actual
> games within games really put time in and make sure these alien casino games
> are innovative and can sink time into them … in certain games you play
> against actual npcs like it could be anyone on the ship on any day."*

**#60 The Wheelhouse** — casino and nightclub, deck 40 outer, next to `#18 The
Pit` so the whole gambling quarter is one walk.

Three games, and they must not be poker with a new hat:

1. **SABACC** — it is canon, it is the one the player will expect, and its
   shifting cards are genuinely unlike a terrestrial game. Against 3 station
   NPCs whose play style comes from their species and temper.
2. **DEJARIK-COLUMN** — the holochess board as a small, fast, perfect-
   information game: five pieces, a shrinking board, three minutes. Deep
   enough to study, short enough to play twenty times.
3. **THE DRUM** — the house game, and mine: a wheel the size of the room whose
   segments are the station's own decks, spun against the station clock, where
   the payout depends on where the *real* station clock lands. Anyone can bet;
   everyone watches the same spin; it runs once an hour whether you are there
   or not.

NPC opponents are drawn from the same rerolling roster as everything else, so
*"it could be anyone on the ship on any day"* is literally true.

### LANE E — MELEE II

> *"at higher levels of upgrading melee you can even deflect bolts back
> (albiet at a much less effective rate and way than with a saber but you can
> do it) so basically you block with your arm/hand suspending one or more
> bolts maybe a foot or two away from you and then sending them back … lore/
> physics whise you are combining the power of the force and your enhanced
> melee skills to do what the lightsaber does."*
>
> *"another melee idea is like a two finger death punch type move where you
> like thrust own your two fingers/poke the enemy … and it completey
> dissassembles them just like your regular dissassmble move but with melee …
> imagine you've like infused your chakra/force into your finger and the
> effect is so strong it dissassmbles droids."*

V15 §3 shipped the Open Hand: five strikes, a chain, four Holocron facets.
V16 adds the top of that branch — two facets past `melee-reach`, on the same
`body` current, both of which need the branch fully built first.

- **THE STILL HAND** (Jedi) / **THE CLOSED FIST** (Sith) — bolt catch. A bolt
  that would hit you is **held**, visibly, a foot from your palm, and thrown
  back on the next press. Worse than a blade in every measurable way: it
  catches one bolt at a time (three at the top rank), the window is tighter,
  the return is slower and less accurate, and it costs Force *and* stamina
  where the blade costs neither. The fantasy lands; the blade stays better.
- **THE ONE POINT** (Jedi) / **THE SEVERING FINGER** (Sith) — a committed,
  slow, long-recovery thrust that **disassembles an inorganic outright** and
  is a very heavy blunt strike with big knockback against anything else. Long
  cooldown, high Force cost, and it must whiff badly if mistimed or it becomes
  the only move anyone uses.

Both reuse machinery that exists: the blade's own deflect solver and the
existing disassemble on droids. Neither is new physics.

### LANE F — CO-OP

> *"when you play coop with your friends and they are on your station they
> should be able to have an apartment the host's game … you should be able to
> visit your friend's apartment/see their companion etc.; it should work with
> up to 4 players … each friend added in gets their apartment spawned in
> somewhere in the residence area … maybe convert some random npcs's house to
> a player's house when they get added in, obviously the station should work

The player has already named the right implementation: **convert an existing
residence.** The residential rows (`#31` Human, `#32` Narn, `#33` Centauri,
`#34` Minbari, `#35` Drazi, `#38` hostel) are already built, already have

That makes four apartments a **lookup**, not four new rooms, and V15's

The larger work is the station in co-op at all: the net layer already carries
avatars, and the station's residents are ordinary `Enemy` bodies on the
player's team, so the honest scope is (a) the host owns the station's
population and clock, (b) guests get the drum and the places, and (c) the four
homes are dressed per-player. **This is the biggest engineering item on the
list and it should come after the systems it shows off.**

## 3. EXTRAPOLATION — WHAT ELSE THE STATION NEEDS

Asked for explicitly. Every one of these is a room that exists or a small
addition, and every one follows the same "a menu function becomes a place"
rule:

1. **A bank, or rather a broker.** If there are credits there must be a place
   they live and someone who takes a cut. `#12 Recruiting office` becomes the
   pay office too: wages, bounties, and the ledger of what you are owed.
2. **A shipping office.** You bought a thing at a counter; where is it? A
   counter delivers to your apartment overnight. `#8 Docking throat` and `#25
   Lost & found` between them are the parcels desk. This also gives a reason

## 4. THE ONE COLLISION, AND HOW IT SETTLES

**Credits are a cross-run currency, and this codebase has a written law
against exactly that.**

`Progress.js`'s header is doctrine — no unlocks, no currency, no cross-run
power — and it is enforced mechanically: `tools/checks/companions.mjs` greps
the companion files for the six words `points|currency|purchase|upgrade|
unlock|buy` and goes red if any appears. `Kennel.js`'s header calls the fact
that a NEW file is invisible to that scan *"a hazard, not a permission."*

So this cannot be sneaked in under a new filename. It has to be argued, and
the player has already supplied the argument in his own list:

> *"maybe cosmetic stuff you buy is permanent and you can also buy powerups
> similar to the stuff you can get in the holocrons but they are temporary and
> do not persist when you die."*

**The law's substance is "a run is won by playing, not by having played
before."** Credits do not touch that if and only if:

- **KEEPSAKES change appearance and nothing else.** No stat, no unlock of a
  capability, no shortcut. The check that guards this measures a bought item's
  effect on the player's numbers and fails on any movement at all.
- **PROVISIONS die with the run**, exactly as the player specified — the same
  contract the Holocron already has, which is why he reached for that
  comparison unprompted.
- **The economy is bounded.** Credits earned per run are capped and the
  interesting rows cost several runs, so hoarding cannot buy an advantage that
  does not exist anyway.
- **The scan is EXTENDED, not evaded.** The new economy files go *onto* the
  currency scan's path list on the commit that creates them, with the two
  categories above named as the exemption and the reason written down. That is
  the difference between an argued change to a rule and a quiet hole in it.

I will not build the shop until this is written into `Progress.js`'s header as
an amendment, because a doctrine that is edited in silence is worse than one
that is broken loudly.
